## 82. The Cool-Down

### EN: Short
A cool-down eases your body from hard activity back toward rest with easy movement and calm breathing. It can feel good and is easy to fit in, but there is no good proof that it prevents delayed muscle soreness.

### EN: Detailed
**Slow down gradually.** After a hard run or ride, walk or pedal easily for a few minutes instead of stopping dead at full effort. After strength work, a short relaxed walk and normal breathing may feel good. Stopping abruptly can sometimes make you light-headed, especially after long, vigorous activity.

**Optional gentle mobility.** You can add comfortable calf, hip, chest or shoulder movements if flexibility is one of your goals or you simply enjoy them. Aim for a mild stretch sensation and don’t force big ranges. Deep stretching right after a tiring session is optional, and painful stretching works against you.

**What the evidence shows.** Light movement may make the first minutes after a workout more comfortable. There is little evidence that a standard cool-down or stretching routine reliably prevents next-day soreness (DOMS). Gradual progression, sleep and recovery still matter. A cool-down does not treat an injury and does not replace medical care.

**Match the session.** A moderate walk can end with one or two easy minutes. After a hard interval session, take several minutes of easy movement and breathing until you feel steady. If dizziness, unusual shortness of breath, chest pain or faintness does not go away, stop training and get medical help.

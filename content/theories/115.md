## 115. Simple Fitness Tests

### EN: Short
Simple field tests give a quick snapshot of your strength endurance, balance or conditioning. They’re most useful when you repeat the same test the same way, and comparing yourself with one score meant for everyone tells you much less.

### EN: Detailed
**Pick a test for your goal.** A controlled push-up test reflects upper-body strength endurance, and a 30-second sit-to-stand test measures repeated lower-body function. A timed plank tests one particular kind of trunk endurance, while a step test or measured walk looks at another side of conditioning. No single test measures total fitness.

**Do it the same way every time.** Use the same push-up depth, chair height, plank position or step height, and keep the warm-up, rest and footwear similar. Stop when your technique breaks down instead of chasing a higher count. Retest after several weeks of training instead of doing max efforts every day. Write down exactly how you did each test so later comparisons mean something.

**Choose a test that fits your level.** If you’re starting from zero, you might count comfortable chair stands using a stable chair. An experienced trainee may need a harder benchmark. If a test causes pain, dizziness, unusual breathlessness or loss of balance, don’t use it until a professional has assessed you.

**Know the limits.** Practice, motivation, fatigue and body proportions all affect field scores. A better score may come from better skill as well as from physical change. Tests you do yourself can’t diagnose cardiovascular, neurological or musculoskeletal conditions.

## 86. Thoracic Spine and Shoulder Mobility

### EN: Short
Your thoracic spine and shoulders take part in reaching, rotating, overhead pressing and many pulling movements. Mobility work can make these motions easier to reach, as long as you also build the strength and control to use them.

### EN: Detailed
**What each region does.** The thoracic spine is the middle section of your back, and it lets you rotate and extend. The shoulder complex includes the ball-and-socket joint and the shoulder blade gliding over the ribcage. Reaching overhead depends on several regions working together, so it tests more than shoulder-joint flexibility.

**Explore comfortable movement.** An open-book rotation drill gently turns your upper body while your hips stay fairly still. Controlled arm circles rehearse shoulder movement, and cat-cow moves the spine through flexion and extension at an easy pace. Move slowly, breathe normally and don’t force the end position.

**Mobility in your lifts.** Overhead pressing may feel smoother when your ribs, shoulder blades and arms move together. In rows, let your shoulder blades move naturally; you don’t need to keep them pinned back the whole time. Aim for smooth control in the exercise you chose. There is no single rigid posture everyone has to copy.

**Mobility plus strength.** Light presses, wall slides, rows and controlled carries build usable range and coordination together. Stretching alone may improve passive flexibility without giving you full control under load. If an exercise feels unstable or uncomfortable, change the grip, angle or range of motion.

**Using TrainUp.** If overhead work bothers your shoulder, add the shoulder as a limitation in Profile, with a note. The plan only picks exercises that fit your limitations. For new, persistent or worsening shoulder pain, see a doctor or physiotherapist instead of relying on general mobility drills.

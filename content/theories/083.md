## 83. Mobility and Flexibility

### EN: Short
Flexibility is the range a joint can move through, and mobility is your ability to control that range yourself. When you pick exercises, the range you can use with control counts for more than the deepest position you can reach.

### EN: Detailed
**Passive and active range.** You might be able to move your arm overhead with help but struggle to lift it there on your own. That points to enough flexibility without the same strength or coordination in that range. Other people have a truly limited comfortable range and may benefit from gradual stretching or a different exercise variation.

**Test the movements you use.** A controlled overhead reach, a comfortable bodyweight squat or a hip hinge can show whether a movement from your workout feels restricted. Compare sides, but don’t force them to match. The point is to spot a practical limit. It does not diagnose the cause of tightness, joint pain or a medical condition.

**Train control in the range you have.** Dynamic arm circles, controlled lunges, thoracic rotations and slow hip movements let you practice ranges under active control. Strength exercises done through a comfortable range and progressed over time may also improve mobility. Static stretching improves flexibility, but on its own it does not always build control.

**No single perfect range.** Body proportions, joint structure, injury history and skill all affect which motions feel comfortable. You don’t need a maximal deep squat or extreme shoulder flexibility to train well. Pick a range where your technique stays stable and pain-free, and build from there gradually.

**Using TrainUp.** If a body area limits your range, add it as a limitation in Profile, with a note. The plan then picks exercises that fit your limitations, level and coordination. Each exercise page in Explore has steps and technique notes. Ask a doctor or physiotherapist about restriction that persists, unexplained pain, or symptoms that affect daily life.

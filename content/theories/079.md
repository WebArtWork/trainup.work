## 79. Jump Rope Skills

### EN: Short
Jump rope is portable conditioning that takes rope timing and small, repeated hops. Get the rope length, wrist control and landing rhythm right before you try long or fast intervals.

### EN: Detailed
**Set up the rope.** Choose a rope that turns smoothly without catching your feet. A common first fit is to stand near its middle and raise the handles toward your lower chest or armpits, then adjust for rope thickness and your skill. Make sure you have plenty of room overhead, to the sides and on the floor before you jump.

**Learn a small basic jump.** Keep your elbows comfortably close to your ribs and turn the rope mostly from the wrists, without swinging your whole arms. Use short, relaxed jumps just high enough to clear the rope, without pulling your knees up high. Land softly, letting your ankles and knees bend; do not try to keep your legs completely stiff.

**Add variations gradually.** Begin with single turns, even if only two or three work before you have to reset. Move on to short continuous sets, then add alternating feet, a boxer step or side-to-side shifts once your timing improves. Tricks such as double-unders come after a reliable basic rhythm.

**Avoid too much impact and frustration.** Skipping repeatedly loads your calves and Achilles tendons, so big sudden increases can make them sore. If the rope keeps catching, it may be too long, too short or badly timed; it does not mean you are unfit. Wear shoes and use a surface with good grip, and avoid slippery tile floors and crowded rooms.

**Using TrainUp.** The plan includes jump rope only when your equipment and your Profile settings for ceiling height, surface, jumping and noise allow it. Keep the intervals short at first. If your skill or the impact holds you back, shadow boxing or marching keep the session going.

## 54. Detraining and Returning After a Break

### EN: Short
Detraining is the partial loss of fitness after you cut back or stop training. A careful return rebuilds your capacity step by step, without expecting your body to handle its old workload straight away.

### EN: Detailed
**What changes during time off.** Aerobic endurance, movement tolerance and lifting performance fade at different speeds. A few missed days may make little difference, while weeks or months without training can lower how much work you can do. Past training may help you relearn skills faster, but “muscle memory” does not remove the need for a gradual return.

**Why you took the break.** Travel or a busy schedule is different from an injury, surgery or a long illness. After a medical event, ask your doctor or another suitable health professional before you go back to hard training. TrainUp can’t tell whether returning after an illness or injury is medically safe for you. If something has changed, update your limitations in Profile before you start again.

**A careful starting point.** After a long break, come back with fewer sets, lighter resistance and more repetitions left in reserve. Starting at around 50–70% of your former training volume is one planning example that suits some people; research does not set this number. See how the first sessions feel before you add work.

**Build over the following weeks.** Practice familiar techniques, leave enough recovery, and add work slowly as movement and soreness settle back to normal. Don’t test your old maximums right away. If the first week leaves you with long-lasting or unusually bad soreness, repeat or cut the workload, even if your schedule says it is time to progress.

**Sources:** [NSCA/CSCCa: safe return following inactivity](https://doi.org/10.1519/SSC.0000000000000477); [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/); [NSCA: safety and resistance-training fundamentals](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/ptq/the-safest-and-riskiest-forms-of-resistance-training/).

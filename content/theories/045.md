## 45. Double Progression

### EN: Short
Double progression is a simple way to move forward in strength training: you add repetitions within a chosen range, then add weight. It takes most of the guesswork out of picking a new load.

### EN: Detailed
**Choose a repetition range.** Pick an exercise, a number of sets and a range, for example three sets of 8–12 repetitions. Start with a weight that lets you reach the bottom of the range with steady technique and a few repetitions left in reserve. The range doesn’t have to be 8–12. Your goal and the exercise decide the limits.

**Earn the load increase.** First, do more good repetitions at the same weight over several sessions. Once every set reaches the top of the range at the effort you planned, add the smallest weight step you have. Your repetitions will usually drop toward the low end again, and the cycle starts over.

**Example.** Week 1: dumbbell rows at 12 kg for 10, 9, 8. Week 2: 11, 10, 9. Week 3: 12, 12, 12 with steady control. Week 4: try 14 kg for 9, 8, 8, if you can handle that jump with good form. These numbers show how it can go. You don’t have to improve every week.

**When to adapt the rule.** A big weight jump may be too hard, especially for small muscles or with limited home equipment. In that case, stay at your current load, add total repetitions, or choose an in-between variation. If a session feels unusually tiring, repeat the last target and leave progression for later. Real progress often goes up and down.

**Using TrainUp.** Your plan gives each exercise sets and a rep target, and during a workout you record the reps you finish in each set. TrainUp does not prescribe weights, so you decide when to add load. When you reach the top of the range in every set, check your RPE and your technique before you go heavier.

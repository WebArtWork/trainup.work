## 97. Warning Signs: When to Stop Immediately

### EN: Short
Some symptoms during exercise can signal an urgent medical problem instead of normal fatigue. Spotting them matters more than finishing the workout or hitting a planned target.

### EN: Detailed
**Emergency symptoms.** Stop exercising immediately if you have chest pain or pressure, faint, get severely short of breath for no clear reason, feel sudden weakness on one side, have a severe new headache with neurological changes, or a serious reaction develops quickly. Chest discomfort with sweating, nausea, lightheadedness or pain spreading to the arm or jaw needs emergency services.

**Other serious warning signs.** Don’t train through sudden severe joint or back pain, marked swelling after an injury, being unable to bear weight, weakness that is getting worse, or new numbness. Back pain together with loss of bladder or bowel control or numbness around the groin needs emergency assessment right away.

**What to do.** Stop, make the area safe and get help. Call your local emergency number for emergency symptoms, and don’t drive yourself if you may be seriously ill or injured. If you are the training partner, don’t try to work out the cause and don’t encourage the person to finish the set. TrainUp cannot judge an emergency, diagnose disease or replace a qualified professional.

**Prevention has limits.** Warm-ups, sensible loads and gradual progression can reduce avoidable stress, but they can’t rule out every medical problem. If you have known heart disease or major risk factors, you may need personal advice from your doctor before starting vigorous exercise.

**Sources:** [NHS: chest pain and emergency symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/chest-pain/); [NHS: back pain and urgent warning signs](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/); [NHS: joint pain](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/joint-pain/).

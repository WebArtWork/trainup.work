## 105. Starting From Zero

### EN: Short
As a beginner, you can get fitter with a small routine you can repeat. In the first month, focus on learning the movements, keeping the effort comfortable and showing up regularly, and leave lifting the most weight possible for later.

### EN: Detailed
**Week one: learn the routine.** Pick two short full-body sessions, and go for easy walks on other days if you feel up to it. Practice the basic squat, hinge, push, pull and core patterns with movements that suit your equipment and your body. A first session might be one or two sets of several exercises.

**Weeks two to four: repeat and observe.** Keep the same exercises, then slowly add reps, a little load or an extra set if you’re recovering well. Small increases you can keep up help you build confidence. Don’t read every new sensation or bit of soreness as a reason to change the whole plan.

**Example.** On Monday and Thursday, do controlled chair squats, supported rows, wall push-ups and a short core hold. Add a comfortable walk on another day. If a movement is too hard, pick an easier version. If it’s very easy, make it harder gradually while keeping control.

**What to expect early on.** Early strength gains often come from better skill and coordination between your nerves and muscles, and physical changes show more over time. A missed workout doesn’t erase your progress. Just pick up again at the next planned session.

**Using TrainUp.** Set your fitness level honestly in Profile, along with the equipment you have and your session length. After each workout, rate your effort (RPE). Your plan changes only when you regenerate it, and never from fewer than 3 workouts. If your last 4 were easy (RPE 5 or less) and complete, the next plan gets a step harder.

**Sources:** [ACSM 2026 practical guidance](https://www.acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Position-Stand-infographic.pdf); [NHS: walking for health](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/walking-for-health/).

## 46. Full-Body, Upper/Lower and Split Programs

### EN: Short
A training split is how you spread your exercises across the week. Full-body, upper/lower and muscle-group splits can all build strength and muscle when the total work, effort and recovery suit you.

### EN: Detailed
**Full-body programs.** A full-body session trains several major movement patterns on the same day. Two or three days a week often works well for beginners and busy people. You practice the main exercises often without needing many gym visits. Sessions can get long if you include too many movements.

**Upper/lower programs.** An upper/lower plan alternates upper-body and lower-body sessions, often over four workouts a week. It leaves room for more exercises and sets, with recovery time between similar sessions. A three-day rotating version also works, and the calendar doesn’t have to repeat exactly every week.

**Muscle-group splits.** A body-part split might train chest and triceps one day, back and biceps another, and legs on a third. These programs make high training volumes easier to fit in. When the weekly work is similar, no split is better for muscle growth than another. A badly designed split can leave some muscles untrained for too long.

**Choose by your limits.** With two days, full-body is a practical default. With three, use full-body or a rotating upper/lower pattern. With four, use upper/lower or two different full-body days. Five days allow many arrangements, but extra visits add little if you can’t stick with them or recover from them. Skill practice and personal preference also play a part. Pick the simplest split that covers your goals, schedule and equipment, and keep an eye on your total weekly sets. A five-day schedule that looks advanced is not automatically better than a three-day plan you keep doing.

## 94. Active Recovery

### EN: Short
Active recovery is easy movement between harder training sessions. Walking, easy cycling and gentle mobility keep you moving without adding a second hard workout.

### EN: Detailed
**What counts.** Active recovery is movement you keep on purpose easier than your normal training. A comfortable walk, a relaxed swim, easy pedaling or a short mobility session can make you feel better and keep up the habit of moving. You don’t need to sweat, get exhausted or reach a high heart-rate zone.

**How easy it should be.** You should be able to talk without effort, and you should finish no more tired than when you started. If your legs burn, you add hard intervals or you get close to muscular failure, that is extra training. When fatigue, symptoms or a busy schedule pile up, full rest can be the better choice. Recovery days are optional, and active recovery does not replace rest.

**Example.** After a hard lower-body session, you might walk for 15–25 minutes and do some relaxed hip and ankle movements. The next day, check whether your normal performance is coming back. Stretching or foam rolling may feel nice, but you don’t need either to clear soreness, and neither guarantees faster recovery.

**What the evidence doesn’t show.** Research has not shown that every form of active recovery speeds up physical repair. Soreness that eases for a while does not prove your tissues have recovered. Never use movement to push through severe or worsening pain.

**Sources:** [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/); [NHS: walking for health](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/walking-for-health/).

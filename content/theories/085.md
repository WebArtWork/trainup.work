## 85. Hip Mobility

### EN: Short
Hip mobility is comfortable movement at the hip joints: bending, extending, rotating and moving the leg to the side. It helps you reach useful squat, hinge and lunge positions, though hip anatomy and comfortable ranges differ from person to person.

### EN: Detailed
**The hip’s motions.** Hip flexion brings the thigh toward the torso, and extension takes it backward. The joint also rotates inward and outward and moves the leg across or away from the body. Everyday movements combine several directions, so a forward toe touch tells you only part of the story.

**Drills to try.** A comfortable half-kneeling hip-flexor stretch explores extension while you keep the pelvis controlled. Gentle 90/90 hip rotations or seated rotation drills practice rotation, and a supported lateral lunge explores inner-thigh (adductor) length and control. Start slowly, and hold onto support if your balance feels shaky.

**Hips in your lifts.** Squats need the hips and ankles to move together. Hinges need hip flexion with a controlled trunk. In a split squat, one hip extends while the other bends. If a deep squat feels uncomfortable, a different stance, a shallower depth, heel support or squatting to a chair may solve it faster than forcing a stretch.

**Everyone’s hips differ.** Hip socket shape, training history, current strength and joint tolerance all affect your range. An asymmetry is not automatically a defect, and the same stretch does not belong in every plan. Stop any drill that causes pinching, sharp joint pain or symptoms that linger, and see a doctor or physiotherapist when needed.

**Tracking progress.** Do a few focused hip drills before sessions where a movement benefits from them. Measure progress by how confident and smooth your squats, hinges and lunges feel, and leave maximal stretching depth aside.

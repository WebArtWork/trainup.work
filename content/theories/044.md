## 44. Progressive Overload

### EN: Short
Progressive overload means slowly making an exercise harder as your body adapts. You need it to keep improving, and you can apply it through repetitions, resistance, movement control, range of motion or a harder exercise, as well as heavier weights.

### EN: Detailed
**What counts as progression.** You might lift slightly more weight for the same repetitions, do more repetitions with the same weight, add a set you can still complete with good form, use more of your range, or move to a harder bodyweight variation. Shorter rest packs more work into the session but may lower your top strength performance, so use it only when it serves your goal.

**Quality you can repeat.** Before you add load, check that the movement is controlled, you finish the planned repetitions, and the effort matches the plan. Technique won’t look identical on every body, but it should look the same for you each time. Pushing on while pain gets worse or the movement gets shaky is not productive overload.

**Example.** Say the goal is two sets of 8–12 goblet squats. You do 10 and 9 repetitions, then 11 and 10 next time, then 12 and 12. Now a small weight increase with fewer repetitions makes sense. If your dumbbells are fixed weights, you can slow the movement down for more control or pick a harder variation.

**One change at a time.** If you raise sets, weight, frequency and exercise complexity all at once, it gets hard to tell where your fatigue comes from. Change the variable that matters most first and give your body time to adapt. Progress slows as you gain experience, and some sessions should simply hold your current level.

**Using TrainUp.** The app records the reps you finish, your session effort (RPE) and any pain you report. You choose the load yourself. When you regenerate your plan after at least 3 workouts, it gets a step harder only if your last 4 were easy (RPE 5 or less) and complete. Any recent pain means no increase, and if 2 of your last 3 were very hard (RPE 9 or more) or less than 60% finished, the next plan is easier.

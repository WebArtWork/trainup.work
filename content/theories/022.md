## 22. Intensity: Load and Percentage of 1RM

### EN: Short
Intensity in resistance training often means how heavy a load is compared with your one-repetition maximum, or 1RM. Experienced lifters can use percentages to set their loads. Beginners can pick a weight more safely from controlled reps and effort, without a maximal test.

### EN: Detailed
**One-repetition maximum.** Your 1RM is the heaviest load you can lift once with a set, consistent technique and range of motion. “80% 1RM” means 80% of your maximum for that exercise. It has nothing to do with body weight. If your squat 1RM is 80 kg, then 64 kg is 80% 1RM.

**What percentages tell you.** Higher percentages usually allow fewer reps before failure, but the exact count varies between exercises, people and training backgrounds. Current evidence shows that heavy loads, around 80% 1RM or more, work especially well for building maximal strength. You don’t need loads that heavy in every workout to improve your overall fitness.

**Estimating 1RM.** Equations can estimate 1RM from a submaximal set. Epley gives estimated 1RM = weight × (1 + reps/30), and Brzycki gives weight × 36/(37 − reps). A set of five at 60 kg gives about 70 kg by Epley and 67.5 kg by Brzycki. These are rough numbers, especially for very high reps, for sets that ended in technical failure, or for sets that were not close to your maximum.

**A safer method for beginners.** Choose a weight that lets you do the planned reps with steady form and about two to four good reps left. Go up only when the target feels easy several times in a row. Maximal tests need skill, proper equipment and often qualified supervision, and ordinary TrainUp training doesn’t need them.

**Example.** If the planned eight reps leave you nothing in reserve and your form breaks down, lower the load. If you finish eight and could easily do eight more, make it a little harder next time.

**Using TrainUp.** Your plan gives sets, reps and rest but no weights, so you choose the load. Judge it from the load, the reps you completed and your effort rating, and never work out an exact 1RM from a single easy set.

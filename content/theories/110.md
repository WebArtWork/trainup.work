## 110. Short Workouts for Busy Schedules

### EN: Short
A well-planned 10–20 minute workout can build a training habit and improve your fitness. If you’re busy, short sessions you do often usually work better than an ambitious plan you can’t stick to.

### EN: Detailed
**Protect the essentials.** First decide what the session is mainly for, such as strength practice or aerobic conditioning. Spend a couple of minutes warming up the movements you’ll use, then focus on one or two main exercises. A short session still needs room for safe technique, and enough rest when the loads are heavy.

**Time-saving formats.** Supersets pair exercises that don’t compete, such as rows and squats, and alternating sets cuts waiting time. Circuits can string simple movements together for general fitness. Don’t rush between hard, technical heavy lifts if fatigue starts to cost you control.

**Example.** For a 15-minute full-body session, warm up briefly, alternate two or three controlled sets of chair squats and incline push-ups, then finish with a row or plank. Adjust reps and rest to what you can do now. On another day, a brisk walk or an easy bike ride can support your conditioning.

**Short bouts add up.** Short bouts of activity count toward your weekly total. Make sessions longer only when it helps and fits your life. When longer blocks aren’t possible, regular short sessions can keep your routine effective.

**Using TrainUp.** Set your session length and preferred training days in Profile to match the time you really have, and edit them whenever your schedule changes.

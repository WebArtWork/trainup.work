## 55. Bodyweight Training

### EN: Short
Bodyweight training uses your own body as resistance for strength and movement practice. Squats, push-ups, lunges, planks and pull-ups can take you a long way without buying a full set of weights.

### EN: Detailed
**Benefits and limits.** Bodyweight work goes anywhere, often costs little, and has many options for beginners. It can train balance, coordination, strength and muscular endurance. Some patterns are harder to load precisely without equipment or a reliable anchor point, especially lower-body hinges you want to make steadily heavier and horizontal pulling.

**Make it challenging.** A wall push-up can lead to an incline push-up and then a floor push-up. Squats can progress through more control, pauses and, later, variations that focus on one leg. Planks can get harder with a longer lever before you add time. Make an exercise harder only while you can still repeat your reps cleanly. Piling on dozens of easy repetitions may shift the training effect toward endurance.

**Work with your equipment.** A secure pull-up bar lets you train vertical pulling, and a firmly installed suspension trainer may let you do rows. Don’t assume a household table, door or railing can hold your body weight. If you have no safe anchor, pick another pattern or use the bands and dumbbells you have.

**Example.** A first session might include two sets each of chair squats, incline push-ups, supported reverse lunges, glute bridges and dead bugs. Pick the number of repetitions by how well you control the movement and how hard it feels. There is no single required target. Rest long enough to keep your form.

**Using TrainUp.** If you have no equipment, say so in Profile, and set your floor size and whether jumping is OK. The plan then picks only bodyweight exercises that fit your space, jumping limit and level. Mark a pull-up bar as available only if it is safely installed; otherwise the plan leaves out exercises that need it.

## 28. Exercise Order in a Session

### EN: Short
Exercise order decides which exercises get you at your freshest. Put your priority exercises, technically hard movements and high-force lifts early, then fit simpler assistance, core or conditioning work where it serves the session goal.

### EN: Detailed
**Priority first.** Fatigue builds up over a session, so the movement that matters most for your goal usually goes where you can do it well. Heavy squats, presses and hard pulls often come early in strength plans. New, complex skills also go better with fresh concentration and controlled practice. Avoid saving the technically hardest exercise for a finisher at the end, when you are most tired.

**Compound and isolation exercises.** Multi-joint movements train several muscles together and may take more coordination and whole-body effort than curls or raises. A common order is warm-up, main lifts, secondary lifts, isolation and optional conditioning. It is a practical habit, and some sessions and people will need a different order.

**What the evidence shows.** Recent ACSM evidence shows that doing strength exercises near the start of a session can improve strength in those movements. Muscle growth seems less affected by exercise order across many comparisons. If better pull-ups are your main goal, do them before exercises that tire your arms and back, even if that means squats come later.

**Core and conditioning.** A hard plank or carry right before heavy squats may tire muscles you need to stabilize the squat. Easy mobility and activation work can come first, and harder core work may fit better after the main lifts. If conditioning is your main goal, cardio can be the main block, with strength work arranged around it.

**Example.** A 40-minute strength session could go like this: a five-minute warm-up, then goblet squats as the main lift, rows, a push-up variation, a hinge, and a short set of side planks. A skill-first session might instead start with a controlled bodyweight pull-up progression before the other exercises.

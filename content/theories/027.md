## 27. Training Frequency

### EN: Short
Training frequency is how often you exercise in a week, counted overall or for one muscle group. A good schedule gives you enough practice and work to progress and still leaves time for recovery and the rest of your life.

### EN: Detailed
**Two different counts.** You can train four days a week and work your legs only once, or train three full-body days and work your legs three times. So the number of sessions and the number of times each muscle is trained are different counts. Weekly hard sets and recovery largely decide how well either schedule works.

**A practical starting point.** For many healthy adults, two resistance-training days that cover the major muscle groups are a reachable health baseline. The 2026 ACSM position stand reports strength benefits from training at least twice a week. For muscle growth, when weekly sets are equal, spreading the same sets over more days has not reliably increased gains.

**Two-day schedule.** Do two full-body workouts with recovery days between them, such as Monday and Thursday. Cover the major patterns, rest enough and focus on finishing the sessions week after week. This often works better than an ambitious five-day plan you can’t keep up.

**Three to five days.** Three days can hold full-body sessions or a rotation of patterns. Four days may suit alternating upper and lower body. Five days can work for experienced trainees with specific goals and sessions that aren’t too long. Training more days doesn’t mean training every muscle hard every day.

**Recovery and progression.** Hard training causes fatigue in the muscles you worked and in the body as a whole. Depending on the workload, a muscle group may need roughly one to several days before another session that hard. The familiar 48-hour gap is a useful starting rule for hard sessions, but it is not a biological stopwatch that applies to every exercise.

**Using TrainUp.** In Profile you set how many days a week you train and which days you prefer. Pick a number you can actually keep. If your sessions keep running long, spread the work over more days instead of piling extra work into each one.

**Sources:** [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/); [WHO strengthening frequency](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9219310/).

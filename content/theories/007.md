## 7. Setting Realistic Training Goals

### EN: Short
A useful training goal is specific, measurable, realistic and tied to a time frame. Pair the result you want with weekly actions you control, so you can see progress even when results come slowly.

### EN: Detailed
**Outcome and process goals.** An outcome goal names the result you want, for example eight controlled push-ups or walking for 30 minutes without stopping. A process goal names the behavior that gets you there, such as two strength sessions every week. Outcomes motivate you, and processes give you the daily plan.

**SMART goals.** A practical goal is specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound. “Get fit” is too vague. Try “do two 30-minute full-body workouts a week for six weeks and build my comfortable continuous walk from 10 to 20 minutes.” Your exact target should depend on your current ability, health and schedule.

**Set a baseline.** Before you start, record something you can repeat: the push-up variation you can do with good form, the load and reps on a familiar movement, or how long you can walk comfortably. Compare like with like. Many beginners improve noticeably in the first weeks, but strength and body composition changes vary a lot, so nobody can reliably promise a specific rate of muscle gain or fat loss.

**Plan for interruptions.** Decide on a minimum version of the goal for busy weeks, such as a shorter workout in place of a skipped session. Don’t set several aggressive targets at once. If a goal is so hard that it needs unsafe speed or too much training volume, scale it down.

**Review and adjust.** Reassess about every four to eight weeks. If you stick to the plan but progress is slow, change one training variable or give yourself more time. If you keep missing sessions, fix the schedule before you add exercises.

**Using TrainUp.** Your goal, training days and session length are in Profile. During each workout you check off sets and record your reps, and afterward you rate your effort. History then shows whether your process goal is happening week by week.

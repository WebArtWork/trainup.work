## 38. Vertical Pulling

### EN: Short
Vertical pulling brings your elbows down from an overhead position, as in pull-ups, chin-ups and lat pulldowns. It mainly works the lats and upper back, with a lot of help from the arms and trunk.

### EN: Detailed
**Pull-up basics.** Use a secure bar and a grip that feels comfortable for your shoulders and hands. Set your trunk, pull until your upper body reaches a consistent height you can manage, and lower with control. The starting shoulder position and range depend on your strength and comfort. You don’t have to start every rep from a full passive dead hang.

**Progressions.** Beginners can practice supported hangs, shoulder blade control drills, band-assisted or machine-assisted pull-ups, and then slow lowering reps. A pulldown trains a similar action with resistance you can adjust. Once you can do several controlled pull-ups, add reps before you add weight. Don’t push lowering reps so hard that your grip or shoulders lose control.

**Pull-ups and pulldowns.** In a pull-up your body moves toward a fixed bar, which takes a lot of whole-body stability. In a pulldown the handle comes to you, and the load can change in small steps. Both build pulling strength, and you don’t need either one to get generally fit. Chin-ups use an underhand grip and often feel different at the elbows and shoulders.

**Safety and setup.** Make sure a home bar is installed correctly and rated for your weight and for moving on it. Avoid loose doorframe mounts and beams no one has tested. Don’t force behind-the-neck pulldowns or swing your body to squeeze out extra reps. Pick a grip you tolerate, and stop if you feel unusual pain.

**Using TrainUp.** In Profile you say whether a pull-up bar is safely installed. If it isn’t, your plan leaves out pull-ups and picks other pulls that fit your equipment, such as a pulldown or a horizontal pull. Don’t improvise an anchor. Note your assistance, grip and range, because each one changes how hard the exercise is.

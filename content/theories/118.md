## 118. Myth: Strength Training Makes Women Bulky

### EN: Short
Resistance training doesn’t automatically make women look very muscular. The usual results are more strength and better muscle function, and how much visible muscle you gain depends on training, time, nutrition and your own biology.

### EN: Detailed
**What makes muscles grow.** Muscles grow in response to repeated mechanical loading and enough recovery. Big changes usually take months or years, not a few lifting sessions. Being strong and looking very muscular are different outcomes, and you can pick training goals that match what you want.

**What comparison studies show.** A 2025 meta-analysis found that women and men gained broadly similar relative muscle size on comparable resistance-training programs, though men’s average absolute gains were somewhat larger. That’s no reason to discourage women from lifting. It shows women can gain real muscle and strength.

**Train for what you want.** Some people want visible muscle growth. Others care more about everyday strength, bone health, sport performance or confidence. Your training volume and exercise choice can follow those priorities. Strength training doesn’t require bodybuilding routines or heavier weights every day.

**Example.** Two full-body sessions a week with squats, rows, presses and hinges can improve strength and function. Track your performance, and don’t worry that a single workout will suddenly change how you look.

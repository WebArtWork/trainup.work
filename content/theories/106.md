## 106. Training After 50

### EN: Short
You can still build strength and fitness after 50, and regular exercise helps you stay mobile and independent. Base your plan on your health, training history, balance and recovery. Being over 50 doesn’t mean you need the same easy routine as everyone else your age.

### EN: Detailed
**Why it matters.** With age, muscle mass, strength, power and balance often change, though the pace varies a lot between people. Resistance training can make everyday tasks easier, such as standing up from a chair, climbing stairs and carrying shopping. Aerobic activity supports cardiovascular health and stamina.

**Train several abilities.** A practical week mixes strength training, moderate aerobic activity and some balance practice. Official guidance for adults 65 and older specifically recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week, muscle strengthening on two days, and balance work. In your fifties, follow the general adult activity guidelines.

**Choose useful movements.** Sit-to-stands, supported step-ups, rows, presses and carries can all be scaled to where you are now. Some healthy older adults benefit from supervised power exercises done with control and fast intent. That doesn’t mean jumping into risky high-impact exercise without preparing for it.

**Progress with feedback.** Make exercises harder as your skill and strength improve, but expect recovery to depend on health conditions, sleep and past training. Get professional advice for new dizziness, chest symptoms, unexplained pain or balance problems. Your age is not a diagnosis and can’t replace a proper assessment, and on its own it is no reason to rule out heavier exercise.

**Sources:** [CDC: older adult physical activity](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/older-adults.html); [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/).

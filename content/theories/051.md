## 51. Progressions and Regressions

### EN: Short
A progression makes an exercise harder, and a regression makes it easier. Both are normal coaching tools for matching an exercise to your strength, technique and recovery.

### EN: Detailed
**Variables you can adjust.** Leverage, resistance, support, range of motion and tempo all change how hard an exercise is. An incline push-up is generally easier than a floor push-up because your arms hold up less of your body weight. A light dumbbell makes a squat harder, and holding on to a stable support makes a lunge easier to balance.

**Push-up examples.** Start at a wall or a sturdy high surface, then lower your hands to a secure bench or counter as you get stronger. Move on to floor push-ups and later, if useful, a feet-elevated or loaded version. A knee push-up and the various incline push-ups are not all the same difficulty, so test which one lets you do controlled repetitions.

**Squat and pull examples.** A chair squat can lead to a bodyweight squat without support and then a goblet squat. For vertical pulling, a lat pulldown or assisted pull-up can come before unassisted repetitions. Write down any change in grip, assistance, range or load, so you know what a better number in your log really means.

**When to change.** Move up when you reach your target range with steady technique and a moderate effort. Move down if the exercise makes your body twist or shift a lot to get through it, if you keep failing reps, or if the balance demand feels unsafe. If something feels uncomfortable, stop and reassess. Pain that persists or feels unusual needs a check by a doctor or physiotherapist.

**Using TrainUp.** Your plan picks exercises that fit your level, coordination, equipment, ceiling height, floor space and any limitations you set. When you regenerate it, it changes one step at a time. It gets harder only after your last 4 workouts were easy (RPE 5 or less) and complete with no recent pain, and easier when 2 of the last 3 were very hard (RPE 9 or more) or less than 60% finished.

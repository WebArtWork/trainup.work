## 101. Training With Sensitive Shoulders

### EN: Short
How your shoulder feels can depend on the pressing angle, your grip, the range of motion and the load. Picking variations you tolerate helps you stay active, but shoulder pain with no clear cause, or pain that keeps going, needs a professional assessment.

### EN: Detailed
**Why shoulders vary.** The shoulder has a wide range of motion and relies on a lot of muscle to stay stable. Reaching overhead, deep pressing positions and fast movements can feel very different from one person to the next. A movement that is restricted or painful tells you something about which exercises to choose. It doesn’t prove you have a particular injury.

**Adjust the exercise.** If a barbell overhead press bothers you, try a neutral-grip dumbbell press, a supported row or a wall push-up. Change the depth or direction before you add resistance. Pulling exercises are a good partner for pressing, but no fixed push-to-pull ratio cures shoulder pain.

**Control and gradual exposure.** While you learn a movement, use slow, controlled reps, a comfortable range and light loads. Building shoulder strength may take gradually harder work, but that progression shouldn’t make your symptoms worse. Forcing a stretch through pain or testing heavy overhead lifts again and again won’t get you there faster.

**Warning signs.** Get medical advice promptly for severe pain after a fall, an arm you can’t move, a visible deformity, major swelling or new numbness that doesn’t go away. Symptoms that don’t improve also need a professional to look at them.

**Using TrainUp.** Add your shoulder under limitations in Profile, with notes, and keep your equipment list up to date. The plan then picks only exercises that fit your limitations, equipment, level and coordination. If an exercise hurts during a workout, tap “I feel pain” to end it at once. It stays out of your plans until you resume it from Profile. Swapping exercises this way doesn’t treat impingement, rotator-cuff disease or any other specific condition.

## 16. Breathing During Exercise

### EN: Short
Breathing supports movement, helps manage pressure in your trunk and changes with how hard you work. For most beginners, a steady rhythm and breathing out during the harder part of each rep is a practical way to avoid holding your breath when you don’t need to.

### EN: Detailed
**Breathing in resistance exercise.** For an ordinary push-up or squat, try breathing in during the easier, lowering phase and breathing out during the effort. Aim for a pattern you can repeat without gasping or losing control. There are exceptions: demanding lifts may use a short, deliberate breath hold to stiffen the trunk, under skilled supervision.

**Bracing and breath-holding.** Coordinated abdominal tension can help stabilize the spine. A Valsalva maneuver (a forceful exhale against a closed airway) can cause large short-term changes in blood pressure and circulation. It is not for everyone, especially people with hypertension, cardiovascular disease or other relevant medical concerns.

**Breathing during cardio.** As intensity rises, you breathe more to bring in oxygen and clear carbon dioxide. You don’t need to match every footstep to an exact inhale and exhale count. A natural, comfortable pattern is fine for most easy activity, and during hard intervals you can let your breathing speed up as much as it needs to.

**Example.** On a set of ten goblet squats, breathe in before you lower, stay gently braced, and breathe out as you stand. If you feel dizzy, stop and rest. Don’t push through it. On an easy walk or ride, being able to talk is a good check that your effort is still moderate.

**Common mistakes.** Holding your breath through many reps, forcing a very slow pattern during hard cardio, and mistaking loud breathing for a good workout all get in the way of controlling your intensity.

**When to get checked.** If you keep getting short of breath for no clear reason, feel faint or have chest symptoms, see a doctor. A different breathing technique won’t fix these.

**Sources:** [CDC intensity and breathing](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/); [American Heart Association exercise guidance](https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/exercise-and-physical-activity/).

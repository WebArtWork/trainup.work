## 9. Major Muscle Groups

### EN: Short
Your major muscle groups work together to move and stabilize your body, so training them takes more than isolating single muscles. Knowing what each group does helps you build balanced sessions and see why one movement can work several areas at once.

### EN: Detailed
**Upper body.** The pectorals help push the arms forward or across the torso, as in push-ups and chest presses. The latissimus dorsi and other back muscles help pull the arms and control the shoulder blades in rows and pull-ups. The deltoids help raise and move the arms. The biceps flex the elbow and assist some pulling movements, and the triceps extend the elbow during presses.

**Lower body.** The quadriceps extend the knee and help with squatting, stepping and lunging. The gluteal muscles extend or stabilize the hip, and hip hinges and step-ups can challenge them. The hamstrings flex the knee and assist hip extension. The calf muscles produce ankle plantarflexion for walking, running and calf raises. The hip adductors and abductors help control leg position and keep the pelvis stable.

**Trunk (the core).** The rectus abdominis, obliques, deeper abdominal muscles, spinal extensors and several muscles around the pelvis help pass force along and resist it. Planks, carries and controlled hinges challenge trunk stability in different directions. Visible “abs” are only one part of this system.

**Overlap between exercises.** A squat works more than the quadriceps, and a row works more than the lats. Grip, torso angle, range of motion and your own anatomy change which muscles do more of the work. Organize a beginner’s week around squats, hinges, lunges, pushes, pulls and core work, and don’t assign each workout to a single muscle.

**Using TrainUp.** Each exercise page in Explore lists its movement pattern and the muscles it works, which helps you cover the whole body with a small number of exercises. Isolation work can fill a specific gap, but it does not replace learning the basic multi-joint movements.

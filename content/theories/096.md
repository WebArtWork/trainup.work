## 96. Pain or Discomfort: How to Tell the Difference

### EN: Short
Exercise often brings muscular effort, fatigue and short-lived soreness, but pain that is sharp, getting worse or comes with nerve symptoms needs attention. A simple symptom check helps you choose a safer next step without trying to diagnose anything.

### EN: Detailed
**Normal training sensations.** Muscles can feel warm, tired or burning during repeated contractions. Delayed-onset muscle soreness may show up a day or two after unfamiliar work. These feelings are usually different from sudden stabbing pain, a joint that feels unstable, symptoms that spread down a limb, or discomfort that stays or gets worse even after you ease off.

**More than one number.** A 0–10 pain rating can describe how strong the pain is, but it can’t tell you the cause. A traffic-light approach helps you sort what you feel. Green is ordinary effort with steady control. Amber is unusual or rising discomfort, and it means stop or change the exercise. Red is severe or alarming symptoms that need medical assessment.

**What to do.** Stop the movement that provokes it. Reduce the load or range only if that feels comfortable, and don’t keep retrying to prove you can tolerate it. New swelling, numbness, clear weakness or symptoms after a fall need a closer look. Pain has many possible causes, and an app can’t name your injury.

**Example.** Burning in your thighs near the end of a set of step-ups is likely muscular effort. A knee that suddenly locks painfully, or that you can’t put weight on, is a different matter, and changing the rep count won’t solve it.

**Using TrainUp.** If something hurts during a workout, tap “I feel pain”. The exercise ends at once and stays out of your plans until you resume it from Profile. After a workout you can report pain or discomfort with the body area and exercise, and you can update your limitations in Profile. TrainUp does not give rehab programs. See a doctor or physiotherapist for pain that persists, is severe or worries you.

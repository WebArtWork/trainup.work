## 93. Daily Readiness and Stress

### EN: Short
Readiness is how prepared you feel and how well you perform going into a session. Poor sleep, illness and life stress can change how much training is right for you that day, even when the written plan stays the same.

### EN: Detailed
**Your total stress.** Training is only one part of the stress you carry. Poor sleep, travel, a demanding job, emotional strain, heat and recent illness can all affect your concentration, coordination and how hard the work feels. Readiness is not a diagnosis, and no single score captures it.

**A simple check-in.** Before a workout, notice your sleep, energy, motivation, soreness and any new symptoms. Compare them with what is normal for you, not with someone else’s numbers. The warm-up gives you a second check: if movements feel unexpectedly hard or your control is off, a demanding session may need adjusting.

**Adjusting on a low day.** On a low-energy day, lower the load, shorten the session, pick simpler exercises or go for an easy walk instead. On a normal day, do the plan as written. Don’t turn every brief dip into a rest day. Trends across several sessions tell you more than one rating.

**Illness and safety.** Fever, illness that affects your whole body, chest symptoms or unusual breathlessness are reasons to skip hard training and get medical advice. The popular “neck check” is only a rough rule of thumb and does not clear you to exercise while sick.

**Using TrainUp.** TrainUp does not prescribe weights, so you choose the load each day. Your plan changes only when you regenerate it, based on the effort (RPE) you rated after each session, any pain you reported and how much of each workout you finished.

**Sources:** [CDC: sleep recommendations](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/); [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/); [NHS: chest pain and emergency symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/chest-pain/).

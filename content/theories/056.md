## 56. Dumbbell Training

### EN: Short
Dumbbells are versatile free weights for presses, rows, squats, hinges and single-leg exercises. Each arm moves on its own and you can change your grip, but you need to pay attention to balance, handling and storage.

### EN: Detailed
**Advantages.** Each hand carries its own load, which makes one-sided work practical and allows grip angles a fixed bar may not. A one-arm row or single-arm press can show differences in control between your sides. Dumbbells often fit small spaces better than a full barbell setup, though the jumps between weights may be large or the choice limited.

**Choose your weight.** Pick a pair or a single dumbbell that lets you reach your target repetitions with steady movement and a few repetitions left in reserve. As a beginner, you may need different loads for rows, curls and presses. If the next weight up is too heavy, first add repetitions, range or control with the lighter one.

**Get into position safely.** For a bench press, sit with the dumbbells held securely near your thighs, lie back under control, and move the weights into place without throwing them. For standing lifts, pick the weights up with your legs and trunk set in a stable position. Don’t drop weights where they could bounce into your feet, damage the floor or hit someone.

**Common uses.** Goblet squats and Romanian deadlifts with dumbbells train the lower body, supported rows and presses cover the upper body, and carries build grip and trunk control. If you have only one dumbbell, use it for one-sided movements.

**Using TrainUp.** In Profile, enter the dumbbell weights you own. Your plan picks exercises that fit your equipment, space and ceiling height. It does not prescribe weights, so you choose the load. If you note your loads after a workout, write them the same way each time, such as “12 kg per hand.”

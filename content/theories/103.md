## 103. Coordination Difficulty and Safe Exercise Choice

### EN: Short
Coordination difficulty is how much balance, timing and movement control an exercise asks of you. Matching it to your experience and how tired you are lets you practice good technique before you add speed or load.

### EN: Detailed
**How complex an exercise is.** A supported machine row moves along a stable path. A single-leg Romanian deadlift asks for balance, trunk control and hip coordination, and a kettlebell swing adds speed and timing. You can be very strong and still be new to one of these skills.

**Go from predictable to challenging.** Start with stable positions and slower reps, and learn one new skill at a time. Then make the exercise harder by taking away support, changing leverage, increasing range or adding controlled speed. A progression should keep the movement’s purpose and avoid changing every demand at once.

**Fatigue changes coordination.** An exercise you did well early in a session can get shaky after many reps. Do high-skill exercises earlier, rest longer when you need to, and end a set when your balance or control slips. Complex ballistic drills are usually a poor first choice for a tired beginner.

**Example.** If you’re learning lunges, you might start with split squats while holding a rail, move on to unsupported reverse lunges, and try walking lunges later. A faster variation isn’t automatically better.

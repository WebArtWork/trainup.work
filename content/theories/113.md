## 113. Measuring Conditioning Progress

### EN: Short
Your conditioning is improving when you can keep going longer, cover a distance faster or do the same task with less effort. Repeating simple tests under similar conditions makes endurance trends easier to read.

### EN: Detailed
**Pick a repeatable measure.** Good options include how far you walk in a set time, how long a familiar route takes, whether you finish a set interval session, or your heart rate at a comfortable fixed pace. Session RPE adds to these numbers, especially if you don’t have a heart-rate monitor.

**Keep test conditions similar.** Temperature, hills, sleep, caffeine, equipment and recovery all affect performance. When you can, compare the same treadmill setting, cycling resistance or outdoor route. A lower heart rate doesn’t always mean you’re fitter, because medication and measurement error can also change the reading.

**Example.** A beginner starts by alternating two minutes of walking with one minute of gentle jogging. Weeks later, they finish more jogging intervals at a similar effort. Someone else rides the same cycling route and it feels easier, even though their average speed stays the same.

**How often to test.** A planned check every few weeks shows trends while your normal sessions stay focused on training. A sudden drop in performance may come from fatigue or illness, and it doesn’t have to mean you’ve lost conditioning for good.

**Using TrainUp.** After each workout you rate the effort from 1 to 10, and History shows your average effort and a 4-week chart. Look at the trend over weeks. A single heart-rate or speed number can’t diagnose your cardiovascular health or guarantee it.

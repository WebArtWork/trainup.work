## 43. Principles of Training

### EN: Short
Good training follows a few connected principles: specificity, progressive overload, individualization, recovery, consistency and reversibility. They are a more reliable guide than copying an advanced athlete’s routine.

### EN: Detailed
**Specificity and overload.** Specificity means your body adapts to what you practice: lifting improves lifting strength, and cycling develops cycling endurance. Overload means the challenge has to be big enough to cause adaptation. You don’t need a heavier weight every session. One more controlled repetition or cleaner execution also counts as progress.

**Individualization and progression.** People respond differently depending on experience, capacity, schedule, environment and goals. A beginner may improve with two short sessions a week, while an experienced lifter may need training spread out more carefully. Progression means making things harder once you have shown you are ready. Faster is not the aim.

**Recovery, consistency and reversibility.** You adapt through repeated training and the recovery between sessions. Sleep, food and a schedule you can keep all decide whether you can carry on. Reversibility means some benefits fade when you stop training, at a rate that depends on you and on the ability in question. Missing one session does not wipe out your progress.

**Variation with a reason.** Changing an exercise can stave off boredom or work around missing equipment, but if you replace every movement each week, you can’t tell whether you are improving. Keep your main patterns the same for several weeks and change accessories or loads when you have a reason. The 2026 ACSM position stand indicates that many different programs work as long as you keep training and keep pushing your effort forward.

**Using TrainUp.** Your goal lives in Profile, each workout records the sets and reps you finish, and you rate the session’s effort (RPE) afterward. When you regenerate your plan, it changes by one step at a time based on that record, so a generic plan does not stay fixed for you. If you adjust things on your own, change one or two variables at a time.

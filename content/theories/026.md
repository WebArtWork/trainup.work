## 26. Timed Sets and Holds

### EN: Short
Timed sets and static holds measure effort in seconds instead of reps. They suit planks, wall sits, hangs and conditioning intervals, as long as your position or pace stays the same for the whole time.

### EN: Detailed
**What duration measures.** A 30-second plank, a 40-second wall sit and a 20-second fast cycling interval all use a timer, but they train different things. Static holds produce force without much joint movement, while timed dynamic sets repeat a motion. The same time can feel very different depending on position, leverage, load and intensity.

**Quality over survival.** In a plank, stop when you can no longer keep your trunk controlled, even if the timer hasn’t finished. In a wall sit, your knee angle and foot position set the difficulty. In a hang, grip and shoulder position matter. Holding a worse position for longer may do less for you than a shorter hold you can repeat in the same position.

**Ways to progress.** Start with a time you can repeat well, such as 15–20 seconds. Then add a few seconds over several sessions, add a set, or move to a slightly harder variation. Change one main thing at a time. Once a hold is very long, adding resistance or a harder lever usually does more than chasing several minutes.

**Timed conditioning sets.** A 30-second work interval followed by 60 seconds of rest is not automatically high-intensity interval training. The intensity depends on your pace or effort during the work. If you are a beginner, choose a speed you can control and enough rest to keep moving well.

**Example.** Do three 20-second side planks per side with 40–60 seconds of rest. When every hold stays steady, move up to 25 seconds. If your hips drop at 12 seconds, shorten the hold or switch to a knee-supported side plank.

**Using TrainUp.** Timed exercises in your plan come with a hold time, and the workout screen has a hold timer. If you change the variation, write it in your workout notes, and don’t compare times from different body positions as if they were the same test.

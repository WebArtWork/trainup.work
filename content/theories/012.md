## 12. Joints and Range of Motion

### EN: Short
Joints move within ranges shaped by anatomy, muscle control, flexibility and comfort. Training through a useful range you can control usually builds ability that carries over better than forcing everyone into the same positions.

### EN: Detailed
**Joints and how they move.** The elbow works mainly like a hinge, allowing flexion and extension. The shoulder and hip are ball-and-socket joints that can move in several directions. The spine has many joints, and their combined movement helps with positioning and passing load. Real joint motion is never quite as simple as a textbook drawing.

**Range of motion (ROM).** Active ROM is the range you can reach with your own muscles. Passive ROM is the range you can reach with outside help, and it may be larger. Limited active range can come from weakness, skill, stiffness, pain or a mix of these, so “tight muscles” are not always the whole story. Body proportions and joint structure also affect how deep an exercise feels comfortable.

**Full and partial reps.** A full rep uses the planned, safe range for that exercise. It does not mean forcing the joint to its anatomical limit. Evidence supports training through a complete range you can control for strength in many settings, and partial reps can help when they are planned on purpose. A pain-free shorter range may be a better choice than a deep, painful one.

**Practical checks.** In a squat, watch whether your feet stay in contact with the floor, your trunk stays controlled and your knees and hips move comfortably. In overhead pressing, check whether your arms reach overhead without a big arch in your lower back. If a movement gets uncontrolled at the bottom or top, change the depth, the grip or the exercise variation.

**Expanding your range.** First find the range you can control, then repeat it reliably, then expand it if that is useful and comfortable. Range is one training variable among others, such as load, reps and stability.

**Using TrainUp.** If movement in a body area is limited, add it as a limitation in Profile, with notes, so the plan picks exercises that fit. TrainUp can’t tell whether restricted movement comes from a medical issue. If a limitation is painful and doesn’t go away, have a doctor or physiotherapist assess it.

## 1. How Training Changes the Body

### EN: Short
Training is a controlled challenge that pushes your nervous system, muscles, heart and other tissues to get better at the tasks you repeat. Progress comes from pairing that challenge with enough recovery and raising the demand gradually, not from making every workout exhausting.

### EN: Detailed
**The adaptation cycle.** A workout is a stimulus. Your muscles produce force, your heart and blood vessels deliver more oxygen, and you practice movement skills. Right afterward, fatigue can lower your performance for a while. During the recovery that follows, your body repairs, refills its stores and adapts. You notice the result over weeks and months of repeated cycles.

**Specific adaptations.** The SAID principle (Specific Adaptation to Imposed Demands) means your body adapts mainly to what you practice. Heavy squats improve force production in squat-like patterns, and brisk walking mainly trains aerobic capacity. General fitness combines several kinds of challenge. Early strength gains often come from better coordination and nervous-system recruitment, while noticeable muscle growth usually takes longer.

**Supercompensation.** A popular diagram shows fitness dropping after training, recovering, then briefly rising above where it started. It is a useful teaching picture, but it is not a precise timetable or a universal biological law. Different systems recover at different rates, and you don’t need to time each session to a mythical peak.

**Example.** A beginner does two moderate full-body workouts a week. After several sessions, the same exercises feel easier. They add one or two reps, a little weight or another carefully chosen challenge while keeping their technique. If they keep stacking hard sessions without recovery, their progress may stall.

**Using TrainUp.** Pick a primary goal in Profile, check off your sets during each workout and rate your effort afterward. History shows a 4-week chart, so look at trends across several weeks. Before you make training harder, think about your sleep, soreness and how consistent you have been.

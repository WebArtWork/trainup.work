## 18. Muscle Growth (Hypertrophy)

### EN: Short
Muscle hypertrophy is an increase in muscle size that comes from repeated training and recovery. Mechanical loading is a major stimulus, and training volume, effort, enough nutrition and sleep affect how much you can adapt.

### EN: Detailed
**What grows.** Resistance exercise triggers signaling pathways and remodeling of muscle proteins. Over repeated sessions, the balance between building and breaking down muscle proteins can tip toward larger muscle fibers. This takes weeks to months. Early changes in appearance or body weight can also come from fluid, glycogen or measurement variation.

**Why tension matters.** Hard muscle contractions put the fibers under mechanical tension. Both heavier and lighter loads can support growth when you do the sets with enough effort and good technique. Training with very light resistance, far from fatigue, may not give enough stimulus for noticeable growth.

**Volume and frequency.** Counting hard sets per week is a useful way to organize hypertrophy work. The 2026 ACSM position stand found better growth with higher weekly volume, and the evidence favored at least about ten weekly sets per muscle group for maximizing growth in the relevant study comparisons. That number is not a required starting minimum. Beginners may grow with less, and recovery limits differ from person to person. Frequency mostly helps you spread the needed work across the week.

**Recovery and nutrition.** Enough dietary protein and energy support adaptation, and sleep and rest help you handle repeated training. TrainUp gives general exercise education and does not give personal diet plans or calorie targets. Genetics, training age and health also affect how fast visible gains appear.

**Example.** Start with a moderate mix of squats, hinges, pushes and pulls. Log your weekly sets and reps. If your performance improves, soreness stays tolerable and you recover well, slowly add reps or a little load before you add more sets.

**Common misconception.** Soreness does not measure muscle growth, and you don’t need to reach failure on every set. Programs that work are built on hard training you can repeat, not on maximal exhaustion.

**Sources:** [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/); [Muscle growth mechanisms review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35389932/).

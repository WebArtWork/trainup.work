## 67. Training Surface and Footwear

### EN: Short
Your floor and shoes affect stability, grip and how hard each landing feels. The best combination depends on whether the movement is mainly lifting, running, jumping or slow mobility work.

### EN: Detailed
**Common surfaces.** A firm, level floor gives steady footing for most strength movements. Thick soft carpet can wobble during single-leg work, and very smooth tiles can get slippery. Outdoor grass or trails may be uneven and damp. A mat makes floor work more comfortable but can slide or compress, so test it before you put heavy loads on it.

**Shoes for the task.** Stable shoes with good grip often work well for squats, hinges and side-to-side movements. Cushioned running shoes can feel comfortable for walking or jogging but may let your foot shift under heavy lifts. Shoes built for particular sports differ, and no single style is best for every exercise or every person.

**Barefoot training depends on the setting.** Training barefoot gives you direct feedback from the ground in a clean, safe space, but it leaves your feet open to dropped weights, sharp objects and other hazards. Some gyms do not allow it. If you have reduced feeling in your feet, balance problems or a medical foot condition, get advice for your own situation before going barefoot, since it is not automatically better.

**Impact and noise.** Jumping and running make more impact and noise than controlled squats or marching. A rubber mat may cut some of the sound, but you still need good grip and a solid floor underneath. Avoid slippery socks, wobbly foam under weights, and mats that wrinkle when you step sideways.

**Using TrainUp.** Set your surface, and whether jumping is OK, in Profile, and the plan picks exercises that suit them. If the floor is slippery or uneven, choose stable low-impact exercises or put the movement off until the surface is safe.

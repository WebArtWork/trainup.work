## 104. Spotting and Safe Bailing

### EN: Short
Spotting means someone helps you when you can’t finish a rep safely. Whether you train with a partner or alone, set up the rack properly, pick a weight you can control and know what you’ll do if a lift fails.

### EN: Detailed
**Prepare before you load the bar.** Check that the bench, rack, bar, collars and floor suit the exercise. In a squat rack, safety pins or straps set at the right height catch the bar if you can’t stand up with it. If you train alone, don’t attempt near-maximal bench presses without safeguards.

**What a spotter does.** Before a barbell bench press, agree with your spotter on whether you want a lift-off, when they should start helping and which commands you’ll use. A spotter has to be able to help and shouldn’t distract you. One inexperienced spotter can’t guarantee your safety under a very heavy load.

**Bailing depends on equipment.** Olympic lifting platforms and bumper plates are made for certain controlled drops. Home floors, crowded gyms and adjustable dumbbells may not be. Never practice improvised emergency drops near people or fragile equipment. Before you attempt hard lifts, learn the escape method for your equipment from a qualified instructor.

**Reduce the need for rescue.** Leave reps in reserve, choose a load you can control, and use a machine or dumbbell version when it suits your setup better. You don’t need to train to muscle failure to make progress, especially on complex lifts.

**Using TrainUp.** TrainUp doesn’t assume you have a spotter, a rack or a safe place to drop weights. The plan picks only exercises that fit the equipment and space you set in Profile, so keep those settings accurate. It doesn’t prescribe weights either: you choose the load.

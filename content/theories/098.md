## 98. Axial Spinal Load

### EN: Short
Axial spinal load is the compressive force that travels along your spine during movement or strength training. Knowing about it explains why some exercises may not suit you if you want options that load the spine less.

### EN: Detailed
**How the load arises.** Standing with a barbell across your shoulders or holding a weight overhead can increase the forces carried through your trunk and spine. Heavy carries and some pulling exercises also demand strong trunk stability. The actual force on the spine depends on body position, muscle effort, leverage and the direction of the resistance, so a bar on the shoulders is only part of the picture.

**Usually safe for healthy people.** Your spine is loaded during everyday movement and can adapt to exercise that is progressed gradually. For healthy trainees, axial loading is not unsafe in itself. It matters more when you have a medical restriction, a new symptom or a personal preference to reduce compression.

**Picking alternatives.** A goblet squat, split squat or supported machine exercise loads the body differently from a heavy back squat. A floor-based core drill can help when standing loaded carries don’t suit you. No substitute removes spinal loading completely.

**Progress carefully.** Learn to brace and control the movement, use loads that match your current ability, and change one training variable at a time. You don’t have to do a high-compression exercise just because it is traditional in strength training.

**Using TrainUp.** If you want to limit spinal loading, add your back as a limitation in Profile, with a note. The plan picks only exercises that fit your limitations. Choosing lower-load exercises does not treat back pain or replace medical advice.

**Sources:** [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/); [NHS: back pain and urgent warning signs](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/).

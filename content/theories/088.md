## 88. Sleep and Training

### EN: Short
Sleep supports physical recovery, learning, mood and steady training performance. Adults generally need at least seven hours a night, and many do best with about seven to nine hours of good-quality sleep.

### EN: Detailed
**Why sleep matters for training.** Sleep helps with tissue repair, nervous-system function, attention and learning movement skills. After poor sleep a normal load can feel heavier, and your motivation or coordination may drop. One short night does not erase your progress, but cutting sleep short again and again may hurt the quality of your training and your general health.

**Length and quality.** Public-health guidance recommends seven or more hours for adults aged 18–60, with age-specific ranges after that. Waking often, an irregular schedule or not feeling refreshed can matter even when your time in bed looks long enough. Needs differ, so the right amount is individual.

**Habits that help.** Go to bed and get up at fairly consistent times, keep the bedroom dark and comfortable, limit bright light before bed and watch late caffeine. Daylight and regular exercise can help keep your sleep rhythm predictable. Don’t judge your sleep by one wearable score alone.

**Training after a bad night.** After a particularly bad night, if your balance or concentration feels off, consider a lighter technique session, an easy walk or a smaller workout. An occasional tough day is not dangerous in itself, but forcing maximal lifts while exhausted again and again does you no good. Sleep trackers give estimates, not clinical measurements. If you have ongoing heavy sleepiness or insomnia, talk to a doctor.

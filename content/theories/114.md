## 114. Body Weight Trends and Measurements

### EN: Short
Body weight and body measurements change for reasons besides gaining or losing fat, such as fluid and digestion. If you choose to track them, trends over time tell you more than single readings.

### EN: Detailed
**Why the scale moves.** Food and drink, hydration, sodium, stored glycogen and other normal processes all shift your body weight from day to day. One high or low reading tells you nothing reliable about muscle or fat. Exercise can change your body composition even when your total weight barely moves.

**Measure the same way each time.** If you weigh yourself, use the same scale and a similar routine instead of random times. A weekly average is often more useful than one reading. Waist and other tape measurements are only comparable if you use the same body landmarks and tape tension.

**Photos are optional.** Progress photos can show changes in posture or appearance, but lighting, camera angle and clothing affect them a lot. You don’t need them if your focus is fitness or strength, and they may not help if you have body-image or eating concerns.

**Fitness and weight are separate.** Moving better, lasting longer and getting stronger are real results whatever your size. If you’re worried about an unexpected weight change or a medical condition, talk to a health professional.

**Using TrainUp.** The Dashboard has a body weight log, and whether you use it is up to you. Read it as a trend over several weeks. TrainUp doesn’t treat body weight as a measure of fitness and doesn’t set weight-loss targets.

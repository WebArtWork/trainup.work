## 3. Training Goals: General Fitness, Strength, Conditioning

### EN: Short
General fitness, strength and conditioning are different priorities, and each one shapes exercise choice, effort, rest and progression. You can train several qualities together, but one primary goal makes a program easier to organize and to judge.

### EN: Detailed
**General fitness.** The aim is broad capability and health: resistance training for the major movement patterns, regular aerobic activity, and some mobility and balance work. Sessions can use moderate rep ranges and whatever equipment you have. Progress might mean more weekly activity, more energy during the day, or better push-up and walking performance.

**Strength.** The priority is producing more force in the movements you choose. A strength program usually puts the main lifts early in the session, raises the resistance over time and uses longer rests, so your technique and performance stay strong. Higher loads help most for maximal strength, but beginners can get stronger without ever testing their maximum.

**Conditioning.** The priority is sustaining effort, recovering between bursts, or improving cardiovascular capacity. Steady walking, cycling, jogging and intervals are all tools for this. How much easy and hard work you do depends on your fitness level, preference, impact tolerance and equipment. Not every conditioning workout should feel like a race.

**Example.** Say you have three 40-minute sessions a week. For a general fitness base, you might use two for full-body resistance and one for cardio. A strength block could keep those two resistance sessions, add a third lifting session and keep some short aerobic activity. A conditioning block might shift toward aerobic work while keeping at least two strength sessions a week when you can.

**Overlap between goals.** Resistance training can improve muscular endurance, and aerobic training can help recovery and health. The goals differ in emphasis and overlap a lot. Pick one primary goal for four to eight weeks, then look at your results before you change priorities. Switching after every workout makes progress hard to judge.

**Using TrainUp.** You set your goal (general fitness, strength or conditioning) in Profile. Whatever the goal, your equipment, space and limitations decide which exercises can go into your plan.

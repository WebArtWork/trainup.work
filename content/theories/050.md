## 50. Choosing Exercises

### EN: Short
Choose exercises that fit your goal, training level, equipment, space and any limitations you have. The best exercise is one you can do safely, repeat regularly and slowly make harder.

### EN: Detailed
**Start with the purpose.** For general fitness, pick a mix you can handle of squats or lunges, hinges, pushes, pulls and trunk work. For strength, put first the patterns and exercises where producing more force matters. For conditioning, add steady or interval activity that suits your level. An exercise that looks impressive but doesn’t serve your goal has little special value.

**Match complexity to skill.** A supported machine row may train a beginner’s back better than an unstable free-weight version. A goblet squat can be easier to learn than a heavy barbell back squat. Free weights, machines, bands and bodyweight are all valid ways to add resistance, and the 2026 ACSM position stand did not name one type of equipment as best for everyone.

**Your space and comfort.** Check that you have enough floor space and overhead clearance, a secure surface, and equipment that is properly installed. If you need to keep noise or impact down, choose step-ups or marching over jumps. If a joint feels uncomfortable, lower the load or range or switch to a pattern that feels fine. If pain persists, get advice from a doctor or physiotherapist.

**Keep some exercises steady.** If you change exercises every session, it’s hard to know whether you are getting better. Keep a few anchor movements long enough to build skill and compare your numbers, then rotate them when your access, preferences or comfort change or you hit a plateau. Variety can keep you interested, but it does not by itself make the training work better.

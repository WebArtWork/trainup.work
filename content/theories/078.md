## 78. Running Technique Basics

### EN: Short
Running technique is about posture, stride timing and getting used to repeated impact a little at a time. As a beginner, you gain more from comfortable, repeatable running and a gradual build-up than from copying one “perfect” foot strike.

### EN: Detailed
**The basic running position.** Run tall and relaxed, with a slight natural forward lean from the whole body and your arms swinging easily. Keep your shoulders loose and do not reach far forward with every step. Your stride should feel coordinated at the speed you choose, without forcing it into a fixed template.

**Cadence and foot strike.** Cadence is the number of steps you take per minute. Some runners may benefit from changing it slightly, but no rule says everyone must run at 180 steps per minute. Heel, midfoot and forefoot strikes spread forces differently, and the evidence does not support making every healthy runner switch to one landing pattern.

**Walk-run intervals.** As a beginner, you could alternate one minute of easy jogging with two minutes of walking for 15–20 minutes, plus a warm-up and cool-down. Over several weeks, lengthen the jogging parts. Build up by time, confidence and how hard it feels, and take walk breaks whenever you want them. The right starting amount depends on how active you have been, how your body handles it and the surface, so there is no fixed schedule everyone must follow.

**Sprints are a separate skill.** Fast accelerations put much more demand on muscles and tendons. Build basic running fitness before you sprint at full speed, and warm up longer when you add faster efforts. Stop if you feel sharp pain or pain that gets worse. Choose shoes and a route that suit the surface and conditions, and if running clashes with your impact limits or you have no safe route, do low-impact conditioning.

**Sources:** [Systematic review: foot-strike technique and injury risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34527750/); [Systematic review of running cadence and injury risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40964543/); [CDC adult physical activity recommendations](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html).

## 87. Recovery Between Sessions

### EN: Short
Between sessions, fatigue fades and your body adapts to the training. A good plan mixes hard workouts with enough rest or lighter activity that you can keep performing week after week.

### EN: Detailed
**How long recovery takes.** It depends on the exercise, total volume, intensity, sleep, fitness and how familiar the work is. After a hard session for a large muscle group, roughly 48–72 hours before another equally hard session is a common planning guide. It is a guideline, and some people need more or less.

**Signs you haven’t recovered.** Soreness that lingers longer than usual, fewer reps at the same load, heavy legs, low motivation and changes in your technique can all point to too much fatigue. One bad session does not prove overtraining. Look for trends across several workouts, especially alongside poor sleep, illness or extra stress in the rest of your life.

**Spread out the hard work.** A typical week might have full-body strength on Monday and Thursday, with easy walks on other days. Training strength more often can work when you spread exercises or muscle groups across the week and keep volume in check. Easy cardio or mobility on a non-lifting day usually fits fine with recovery.

**Help your body adapt.** Aim for regular sleep, enough food and water, small training increases and rest days when you need them. Soreness is not a score for how good the workout was. If your performance keeps dropping, cut volume, choose easier variations or add recovery time. Don’t raise the intensity to make up for it.

**Using TrainUp.** After each workout, rate your effort and report any pain or discomfort. When you regenerate the plan, TrainUp looks at at least 3 workouts. Any recent pain means no increase. If 2 of your last 3 workouts were very hard (RPE 9 or more) or less than 60% finished, the next plan is easier. History shows your average effort and a 4-week chart. If severe symptoms persist, get medical advice.

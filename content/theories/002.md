## 2. Components of Physical Fitness

### EN: Short
Physical fitness is made of several partly independent abilities: strength, power, muscular endurance, aerobic endurance, mobility, balance and coordination. Knowing them helps you pick exercises that match your goal, because different workouts train different things.

### EN: Detailed
**The main components.** Strength is the ability to produce force, and power is force produced quickly. Muscular endurance means repeating or holding contractions. Cardiorespiratory endurance supports longer efforts and depends on your heart, lungs, circulation and muscles. Mobility is the ability to move actively through useful ranges, while flexibility describes how far a tissue or joint goes when it is stretched. Balance and coordination help you control movement.

**Body composition.** The relative amounts of muscle, fat, bone and other tissue can matter for health or sport, but they do not fully describe fitness. Two people of similar body weight may differ a lot in strength, cardiovascular capacity or movement skill. Looks can’t replace testing what you can actually do.

**How the components interact.** A squat needs leg and trunk strength, enough mobility for your chosen depth, and coordination to keep the load under control. A hike draws more on aerobic and muscular endurance, though strength and balance help too. Improving one quality can help another, but it won’t train all of them equally.

**Training by goal.** A general fitness week might include two full-body resistance sessions, moderate walking or cycling, and short balance or mobility work. A strength emphasis uses resistance exercises that get harder over time, with enough rest between efforts. A conditioning emphasis spends more time on sustained movement and intervals, and keeps some strength work.

**Using TrainUp.** Pick a primary goal in Profile and keep doing some work for the other qualities. Measure progress with what fits each one: reps or load for strength, pace and breathing for conditioning, controlled range for mobility. Don’t judge a session by how much you sweat or how many calories you burned.

## 65. Usable Space and Exercise Clearance

### EN: Short
Usable training space covers where you stand and also everywhere your body and equipment travel during each rep. Measuring it helps you avoid collisions and lets TrainUp pick exercises that really fit.

### EN: Detailed
**Measure the footprint.** Note the length and width of clear floor, which is usually smaller than the room itself. A standing curl needs fairly little space, while a walking lunge or a floor exercise may need room to stretch out your arms and legs. Leave extra space around moving weights and think about how the exercise starts and ends.

**Think in three dimensions.** A squat or standing row needs room to the sides for your elbows and the equipment. A push-up or dead bug needs floor space as long as your body. A kettlebell swing needs open space in front, behind and around the bell, and overhead presses or jumps need vertical room.

**Hidden obstacles.** Watch for low tables, hanging lights, sharp corners, heaters, pets, cables, rugs and doors that open into your area. Check that the floor is still non-slip once your mat is down. If you can only just finish one rep without touching furniture, the space is too tight for a full workout.

**A plan for small spaces.** In a narrow space, do stationary split squats in place of walking lunges, and seated or floor-based upper-body work in place of big side-to-side movements. If the room is too small for loaded carries, do anti-lateral-flexion exercises on the spot. Keep the movement pattern and let the specific exercise change.

**Using TrainUp.** Enter your floor size and ceiling height in Profile. The plan then picks only exercises whose movement, including the equipment, fits that space.

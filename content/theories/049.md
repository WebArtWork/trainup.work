## 49. Deload Weeks

### EN: Short
A deload is a short period of lighter training that lets fatigue drop while you keep practicing your movements. You can plan deloads ahead or use one when your normal workload stops feeling like something you recover from.

### EN: Detailed
**What to reduce.** The simplest approach is to do fewer hard sets and stay further from failure for several sessions. If you usually do four sets per exercise, for example, you might do two easy sets each for a week. Cutting volume by around 40–50% is one practical option. Science does not set an exact percentage.

**Keep some training stimulus.** A deload can still include training. Doing familiar exercises at a lighter effort keeps your coordination and routine going. Some lifters keep moderate loads and do fewer sets. Others lower both load and sets when their joints or general fatigue are the worry. The best choice depends on why you need the deload.

**When it helps.** Warning signs can include several unusually hard workouts, falling performance, fatigue that won’t go away, or a growing sense that you aren’t recovering. These signs have many possible causes. Don’t write off illness or medical symptoms as normal training fatigue. If you are new to training, you may simply need an easier regular plan, with no scheduled deloads.

**Planned or as needed.** Some structured programs use a lighter week every four to eight weeks, especially when the workload is high. That timing is one way to organize things. If a moderate program is going well for you, you may not need a formal deload, and if you are under unusual stress, an earlier one may help.

**Using TrainUp.** Week 4 of every TrainUp plan is a lighter deload week. When you regenerate your plan, it gets easier if 2 of your last 3 workouts were very hard (RPE 9 or more) or less than 60% finished. After a lighter stretch, return to normal loads step by step, and watch how you respond. A week off does not guarantee you come back stronger.

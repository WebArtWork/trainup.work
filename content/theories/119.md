## 119. Myth: Cardio Destroys Muscle Gains

### EN: Short
Aerobic exercise and resistance training can work together in one fitness program. Some combinations may slightly reduce certain strength or power gains, but cardio doesn’t automatically cancel muscle growth.

### EN: Detailed
**What concurrent training is.** Strength training challenges how much force your muscles produce, and aerobic exercise improves how long you can keep going. For general health you benefit from both. The so-called interference effect describes cases where combining hard training of both kinds may reduce some gains compared with doing only one.

**What reviews show.** A systematic review found that combining aerobic and strength training did not significantly reduce whole-muscle growth or maximal strength on average. Gains in explosive strength were somewhat more affected, particularly when both kinds of training were done in the same session. The exact outcome depends on volume, exercise type, timing and training history.

**Plan around your priority.** If maximal lifting is your main goal, do your best strength work before hard cardio, or put demanding sessions on separate days when you can. If your focus is general fitness, you can combine easy cardio and strength without elaborate scheduling. Recovery and total workload matter more, and neither kind of training is the enemy of the other.

**Example.** Two full-body strength sessions a week plus moderate cycling or walking on other days can build several abilities at once. A competitive power athlete may be more careful about doing hard intervals right before explosive lifting.

**Using TrainUp.** Choose your main focus as your goal in Profile: general fitness, strength or conditioning. You don’t need separate days for everything, and no single cardio session wipes out your muscle gains.

**Sources:** [Systematic review: combining aerobic and strength exercise](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/); [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/).

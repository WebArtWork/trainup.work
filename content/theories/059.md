## 59. Barbell Training

### EN: Short
A barbell lets you load lifts such as squats, deadlifts, presses and rows a little heavier over time. It is good for building a high level of strength, but you need sound technique, a correct equipment setup and safety precautions.

### EN: Detailed
**Barbell parts.** A typical setup has a bar, plates that fit it, and collars to hold the plates in place when needed. The empty bar can feel heavy to a beginner, and specialty bars have different weights and sizes. Record the total weight you lift, bar included, and not just the plates.

**Why use a barbell.** Small plates let you add load in precise steps, and one bar can train several major movement patterns. Both hands hold one connected bar, though, and that doesn’t suit every shoulder or wrist. Dumbbells, machines or bodyweight exercises may be a better choice when your equipment, comfort or experience is limited.

**Rack and safety setup.** For squats, set the J-hooks at the right height for you, and place the safety arms or pins where they will catch a failed rep while still letting you move through your full planned range. For the bench press, use a competent spotter or safeties that are set up properly. Practice getting into and out of the rack with an empty bar before you lift hard loads.

**Learn before you load up.** Practice the squat, hinge, press or row with light resistance until the bar follows the same path every rep. Secure your plates when needed, check the space around you, and never assume a bench, stand or improvised rack can catch a falling bar. You don’t need to test your one-rep max to build strength as a beginner. Do barbell lifts only when you have the right bar and plates, a rack or other safe setup, and enough space. If you train alone without proper safeties, or have limits on loading your spine, choose another exercise.

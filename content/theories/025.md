## 25. Lifting Tempo

### EN: Short
Lifting tempo is the speed and timing of each phase of a rep. A controlled tempo helps you practice technique and makes workouts easier to repeat. Very slow reps are not automatically better for strength or muscle growth.

### EN: Detailed
**Four-part notation.** Tempo is often written as four numbers: the lowering phase, the pause at the bottom, the lifting phase and the pause at the top. In a squat, 3-1-1-0 means lower for three seconds, pause one second at the bottom, rise in one second, and start the next rep right away. Not every system lists the phases in the same order, so the instructions have to say which notation they use.

**Why control helps.** A slow lowering phase can stop you bouncing, show up balance problems, and make light loads hard enough for skill practice. A short pause can help you feel your position at a sticking point. Lifting fast on purpose may help strength or power work when your technique is safe and the load suits it.

**Tempo and hypertrophy.** Research has not shown that very slow reps reliably produce more growth. The 2026 ACSM position stand found that time under tension was not a consistent factor when different training plans were compared. Going extremely slow can cut the load or total reps you manage and may work against your main goal.

**Example.** If you are learning a hinge, lowering a dumbbell over two to three seconds gives you time to keep the weight close and feel your hips move back. Once the pattern is consistent, a normal controlled speed may be enough. A medicine-ball throw or a jump is meant to be fast, so don’t slow it down.

**Comparing sets.** When you judge progress, compare sets done with a similar range and speed. Adding weight while cutting the lowering phase short may not be a real improvement. Unless a skill calls for a set tempo, focus on steady, controlled reps and your main goal, and don’t count every second.

**Sources:** [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/); [Repetition-continuum review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33671664/).

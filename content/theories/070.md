## 70. Training Outdoors: Weather and Terrain

### EN: Short
Outdoors, the weather, ground, light and equipment all change from day to day. Adjusting how hard you go and where you go makes walks, runs and park workouts safer and easier to keep up.

### EN: Detailed
**Training in the heat.** Heat and humidity can make an easy session feel much harder. Choose cooler hours and shaded routes, start gently, and drink enough for the conditions. The CDC advises stopping and moving to a cool place if you feel faint or weak. Confusion, collapse or suspected heat stroke needs emergency help.

**Cold, wet and windy days.** Wear layers you can take off and cover exposed skin when it is cold. Rain and sweat can make clothing and surfaces dangerously cold, and severe cold can cause frostbite or hypothermia. Ice or poor visibility is a good reason to train indoors that day.

**Check the ground.** Look for holes, loose gravel, roots, uneven pavement, poor lighting and road crossings. Slopes change the load on your muscles and joints. On a new route, start at a comfortable pace, skip all-out sprints, and wear shoes with good grip for the surface.

**Check park equipment.** Benches, rails and pull-up bars may be weathered, loose or not built for dynamic exercise. Look for sharp edges, rust, broken fittings and wobbly supports. Do not hang from anything unless it is clearly built to hold you. Give cyclists, children and other park users plenty of room.

**Check again every time.** Outdoor conditions change, so look again each time you go out. In dangerous weather, switch to an indoor or low-impact session, and choose routes and movements that suit the light, the ground and your experience.

**Sources:** [CDC: exercising in heat](https://www.cdc.gov/heat-health/risk-factors/heat-and-athletes.html); [CDC: preventing frostbite and hypothermia](https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/preventing-frostbite.html); [CDC adult physical activity recommendations](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html).

## 60. Machines and Cable Training

### EN: Short
Weight machines and cable stations give you adjustable resistance and can make exercise setup simpler. They are effective strength-training tools alongside free weights, not a lesser substitute.

### EN: Detailed
**What machines offer.** Selectorized machines often guide the path of the movement and may reduce how much you need to balance. That can help a beginner focus on pushing or pulling with control. Some machines fit certain body sizes better than others, so a guided path is not comfortable for everyone.

**Set up the station.** Adjust the seat height, back support and pads so the working joint lines up reasonably well with the moving parts and you can reach the grip without straining. Do a light trial set before you choose your working load. Keep your hands away from weight stacks, pinch points and moving arms, and lower the resistance slowly. Gyms have different equipment, so check the exact machine or cable station you have, and switch to a free-weight, band or bodyweight option if you can’t adjust the machine to feel comfortable.

**How cables differ.** Cable systems let you pull from several directions, with tension along the line of the cable. They work well for rows, pulldowns, presses and anti-rotation exercises. Cable resistance is not exactly constant at every joint angle, because leverage and the cable’s direction change through the movement.

**Comparing tools.** Free weights ask you to stabilize the load more yourself, while machines can make some exercises easier to learn or to load precisely. Both can build strength and muscle size when your effort and progression are right. A machine does not automatically protect an injured or painful joint. If symptoms persist, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

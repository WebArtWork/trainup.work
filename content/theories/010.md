## 10. How Muscles Work: Concentric, Eccentric, Isometric

### EN: Short
Muscles produce or resist force while shortening, lengthening or holding a position. These concentric, eccentric and isometric actions often happen within the same exercise, and they help explain what it means to control a weight.

### EN: Detailed
**Concentric action.** The muscle produces force while it shortens overall. In a biceps curl, the elbow flexors shorten as the weight rises. On the way up from a squat, several hip and knee extensors work to lift you. “Concentric” describes what a muscle is doing, which is not always the same as whether the exercise looks like an upward movement.

**Eccentric action.** The muscle produces force while it lengthens under load, often to brake a movement. Lowering a dumbbell curl or sitting down into a squat uses eccentric action by the main working muscles. This phase helps strength and muscle growth, but very unfamiliar eccentric work can cause a lot of soreness. Build it up gradually, and don’t exaggerate every lowering phase on purpose.

**Isometric action.** The muscle produces force without much change in its overall length or in the joint angle it controls. Planks and wall sits are clear examples, and your trunk muscles work roughly isometrically when you hold a heavy carry. A hold at one angle is not a perfect substitute for moving through the whole range.

**Roles change with the task.** An agonist is a main contributor to the intended action, and an antagonist opposes it or helps regulate the movement. Synergists assist, and stabilizers control joints or body segments. In a push-up, the chest and triceps do the pushing while the trunk muscles keep you aligned. These roles depend on the movement, so a muscle has no permanent label.

**Putting it into practice.** Lower with control, avoid uncontrolled bouncing, and push or pull through a range you can control. Timed holds are useful when the task needs no movement, and dynamic reps are useful when you want strength through motion. In TrainUp, each exercise is set as reps or as a hold time, and the hold timer tracks your holds. Technique notes and tempo help you do good reps, but moving slower does not always bring faster results.

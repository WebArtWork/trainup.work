## 66. Ceiling Height and Overhead Exercises

### EN: Short
Ceiling height decides whether overhead presses, pull-ups, jumps and rope skipping are practical. A movement can fit while you stand still and become unsafe once your arms, the load or your body move upward.

### EN: Detailed
**Estimate your overhead reach.** Stand on the surface you will train on and reach your arms naturally overhead. Then add the height of the dumbbell, kettlebell, barbell or other implement above your hands. Leave room for controlled movement so the object never stops just short of the ceiling.

**Movement needs more than standing height.** Jumping adds vertical travel, and a skipping rope passes above your head. Pull-ups need room above the bar for your head and room below for your feet, and doorframe bars also need safe clearance where they attach. Ceiling fans, lights, pipes and exposed beams can lower the height you can really use.

**Test the movement empty.** If the ceiling looks high enough, rehearse the movement without load and check the highest point from a safe position. Never test by lifting something heavy toward a low ceiling. Even a short person may need more room than expected, because the implement travels above the hands.

**Practical substitutions.** Under a low ceiling, floor presses can replace overhead presses, and rows can replace pull-ups if the bar’s installation or clearance is unsuitable. For conditioning, choose marching, step-ups or shadow boxing over jump rope or jumping jacks with arms overhead. These swaps may not train the exact same joint action, but they keep the goal of the session.

**Using TrainUp.** Enter your ceiling height in Profile along with your floor size. The plan leaves out overhead and jumping exercises that need more clearance than you have.

## 90. Overreaching and Overtraining

### EN: Short
Overreaching is a temporary drop in performance after you pile up more training stress than your body can recover from right now. True overtraining syndrome is more serious and lasts longer, and one bad workout can’t tell you that you have it.

### EN: Detailed
**Short-term and long-term patterns.** Advanced athletes sometimes use functional overreaching on purpose, and their performance bounces back after enough recovery. Non-functional overreaching causes a longer drop in performance with no useful gain. Overtraining syndrome is a lasting condition with many contributing factors, and it needs a professional assessment that rules out other causes.

**Warning signs.** Repeated drops in performance, fatigue out of proportion to your training, unusual irritability, disturbed sleep, falling motivation and persistent soreness can all be warning signs. None of them is specific to overtraining. Work stress, illness, not eating enough and many medical conditions can cause the same symptoms.

**Cut stress before adding more.** If several sessions feel unusually hard and your performance is falling, lower the volume or intensity for a while, take more recovery, and look at your sleep and overall training load. A lighter week can make sense, but how much to cut differs from person to person. Don’t try to “push through” fatigue that keeps getting worse with one maximal effort after another.

**Avoiding chronic overload.** Increase training gradually, space out hard workouts, and watch the trends in your completed reps, RPE and how ready you feel. Plan easy periods when travel, illness, heavy work or other demands add to your total stress. Extra sessions help only if your body can adapt to them.

**Using TrainUp.** Week 4 of every plan is a lighter deload week. When you regenerate the plan, it changes by one step at a time and never from fewer than 3 workouts. If 2 of your last 3 workouts were very hard (RPE 9 or more) or less than 60% finished, the next plan is easier. TrainUp does not diagnose or treat overtraining syndrome. If the downward trend continues, rest and get professional advice.

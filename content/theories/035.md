## 35. Horizontal Pushing

### EN: Short
Horizontal pushing moves your arms forward from your torso, as in push-ups, chest presses and bench presses. It mainly trains the chest, triceps and front of the shoulders, and it asks for trunk and shoulder control.

### EN: Detailed
**Push-up basics.** Place your hands where they feel comfortable, keep your body in one line from shoulders to hips, lower with your elbows at an angle your shoulders tolerate, and push the floor away. Your shoulder blades naturally move around your rib cage during a push-up. Don’t let your hips sag or push your head forward just to count a rep.

**Choosing a variation.** A wall push-up is often the easiest. Next comes an incline on a sturdy counter or bench, then a knee or full floor push-up. Later you can raise your feet or add load to make it harder. Whether the knee or the incline version is easier for you depends on leverage and comfort. Raising your hands is a simple way to push less of your body weight.

**Bench and dumbbell presses.** On a bench, plant your feet firmly, set a stable trunk and shoulder position, and lower the weights under control. A barbell locks your hands in place, while dumbbells let your grip move more freely but need control on each side. Heavy bench press attempts need a capable spotter or suitable safety equipment.

**Common misunderstandings.** Your elbows don’t have to stay glued to your ribs, and they shouldn’t always flare straight out either. Pick a grip width and elbow path that let you move strongly and comfortably. Your shoulder blades don’t stay frozen in one position across every press, and floor push-ups and bench presses ask different things of them.

**Using TrainUp.** Stick with one push-up variation and record its reps until you are ready to move on. Over the week, balance horizontal pressing with pulling work. If your wrists or shoulders are sensitive, add them as limitations in Profile so your plan only picks pushes that fit.

## 6. Physical Activity Guidelines

### EN: Short
Physical activity guidelines set the minimum weekly activity linked to important health benefits. For most adults, a useful target is regular moderate or vigorous aerobic activity plus strength training for the major muscle groups on at least two days a week.

### EN: Detailed
**Adults aged 18–64.** World Health Organization guidance recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity each week, or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity, or an equivalent mix of the two. It also recommends muscle-strengthening activity for all major muscle groups on at least two days per week. More activity can bring extra benefits, but the right amount depends on your health and capacity.

**What intensity means.** Moderate activity raises your breathing and heart rate, but you can still hold a conversation. Vigorous activity makes it hard to speak in full sentences. Brisk walking or easy cycling count as moderate, and running or hard cycling count as vigorous. You can spread the minutes across the week in sessions that fit your schedule, so one long workout is not required.

**Older adults and young people.** Older adults follow the same aerobic and strength targets. They are also encouraged to do multicomponent activity that emphasizes balance and strength on at least three days per week where possible. For ages 5–17, WHO recommends an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity a day across the week, with vigorous activity and muscle- and bone-strengthening activity on at least three days per week.

**Starting small still counts.** Any increase from inactivity helps. Ten-minute walks, taking the stairs and short home sessions all build toward the recommended totals. Sitting less helps even if you already exercise, and light activity can replace some of that sitting time.

**Example.** A beginner might schedule two whole-body workouts and three 30-minute walks, then slowly lengthen the walks toward the aerobic target. Track walking minutes separately from strength sets. The guidelines are targets for whole populations, so you don’t have to jump to the full amount right away.

**Sources:** [WHO 2020 guidelines](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9219310/); [CDC activity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/physical-activity/getting-started.html).

## 72. Steady-State Cardio

### EN: Short
Steady-state cardio keeps your effort fairly even for the whole session, through activities such as walking, jogging, cycling or cardio machines. It builds endurance without repeated all-out intervals.

### EN: Detailed
**What steady effort looks like.** Your heart rate will not be a perfectly flat line. Pace and breathing can shift with hills, heat or fatigue, while the overall effort stays under control. Choose an activity you can keep going without frequent stops or sloppy technique.

**Pick an intensity.** For many general fitness sessions, moderate intensity feels like work but still lets you hold a conversation. The talk test is often more useful than chasing a set speed or heart-rate number. Trained people can do vigorous continuous work, but beginners usually do better starting gently.

**Add time before speed.** Start with a practical amount such as 10–20 minutes of brisk walking or easy cycling. Lengthen the session in small steps over time, then think about a faster pace or more machine resistance. If you raise time, speed and frequency all at once, it is harder to tell what is causing your fatigue and how well you recover.

**Adapt to the conditions.** Walking and cycling are easier choices if you want less impact, while jogging adds repeated landings. Indoors, machines let you keep settings fairly constant, but outdoors, slopes and weather change how the same displayed pace feels. Use how hard it feels to compare sessions fairly. You are improving when the same route feels easier or a comfortable session gets longer, and you do not need to push every workout to the limit.

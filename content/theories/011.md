## 11. Muscle Fiber Types

### EN: Short
Skeletal muscles contain fibers that differ in fatigue resistance and force production, usually described as type I and type II. Both types help you move, and training improves how they perform without turning you into a completely different “fiber type.”

### EN: Detailed
**Type I fibers.** Often called slow-twitch, they tend to contract more slowly, produce less peak force per fiber and resist fatigue well. They use aerobic metabolism effectively and do much of the work in long or repeated low-intensity activity, such as walking and steady cycling.

**Type II fibers.** These are broadly called fast-twitch and can produce force more quickly and powerfully. In humans, type IIa fibers have a large capacity for both oxidative and glycolytic energy production, while type IIx fibers tend to be faster and tire more easily. The mix of fiber types varies by muscle and by person.

**Recruitment follows demand.** The size principle describes how your nervous system generally recruits smaller, lower-threshold motor units first and adds higher-threshold units as the force needed rises. Heavy lifts recruit high-threshold motor units early. Lighter loads can recruit them too as fatigue builds, as long as the effort gets high enough. That helps explain why muscles can grow across a fairly wide range of loads.

**What training changes.** Endurance training improves your aerobic machinery and fatigue resistance. Resistance and power training improve force production and can build muscle. Some fiber traits change with training, but genetics, training history and age affect the results, so calling someone a “fast-twitch person” or a “slow-twitch person” is misleading.

**Example.** A 40-second bodyweight squat set and a hard five-rep loaded squat stress your neuromuscular system differently. Both can be useful, but the loaded set practices high-force production more specifically. You don’t need to test your fiber mix to build a complete program. Choose reps, rest and conditioning work for the adaptation you want and for how well you move, not for a fiber-type label nobody has checked.

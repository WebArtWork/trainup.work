## 81. The Warm-Up

### EN: Short
A warm-up gets your breathing, circulation, joints, muscles and coordination ready for the work ahead. A short general warm-up followed by a rehearsal of the exercise you are about to do usually helps more than a fixed set of stretches.

### EN: Detailed
**The RAMP framework.** RAMP stands for Raise, Activate, Mobilize and Potentiate. Raise your body temperature and breathing with easy activity, wake up the muscles you will use with controlled movements, move through the ranges you need, and then rehearse the exercise at a gradually higher effort that still leaves you fresh. The phases can overlap. They do not have to be four long blocks.

**A five-to-ten-minute start.** Begin with light walking or cycling. Add controlled bodyweight squats, hip hinges, arm circles or other drills that match the session. Before a hard lift, do one or more lighter ramp-up sets. Before intervals, start with easier versions of the same motion instead of going straight into a sprint.

**Warm up for the task.** An overhead press may call for some shoulder movement and a light pressing set. A run may call for easy jogging and a few gradual accelerations. Heavy or technical work often needs more preparation than a gentle walk. The goal is to feel ready, and an elaborate warm-up that tires you out misses that goal.

**Where stretching fits.** Long passive stretches right before explosive activity can briefly reduce performance in some settings. Short, comfortable stretching is not automatically harmful, but dynamic mobility and gradual practice usually matter more just before strength work, sprinting or jumping. Never stretch into pain to reach some arbitrary depth.

**Putting it together.** Build your warm-up from the movements in today’s session, your skill level and your space: a few minutes of easy activity, then light versions of your first exercise, with more preparation when the work is heavier or faster. Count the warm-up as part of your session time.

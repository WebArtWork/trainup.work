## 99. Training With Sensitive Knees

### EN: Short
Sensitive knees can make some movements, depths or impact levels uncomfortable. Sensible training choices can cut down on avoidable flare-ups, but knee symptoms that persist or are severe need a professional assessment.

### EN: Detailed
**What changes the load on your knees.** Squat depth, step height, balance, speed, resistance and jumping impact all change how hard your knees work. There is no universal rule that your knee must never move past your toes. How far your knee can comfortably travel differs between people and between tasks.

**Practical swaps.** A controlled sit-to-stand, a supported split squat or a low step-up can be easier to learn than a fast jump squat. A shallower or slower version may feel more comfortable in some cases. When impact is a problem, low-impact conditioning such as cycling or walking on level ground can work better than jumping.

**Build up gradually.** Start with a movement and an amount of training your knees tolerate. Add load, depth or reps only while symptoms stay acceptable during and after training. Don’t keep testing a painful position to see whether it has “fixed itself.” General exercise advice is not a diagnosis or a rehab program.

**When to get help.** A very painful, visibly deformed or badly swollen knee, being unable to put weight on it, locking, fever with redness, or symptoms after a significant injury need prompt professional assessment. Problems that persist should be checked too.

**Using TrainUp.** Add your knees as a limitation in Profile, with a note, and set whether jumping is OK. The plan then leaves out exercises that don’t fit. If an exercise hurts, “I feel pain” ends it and keeps it out of your plans until you resume it from Profile. No exercise is guaranteed to be “knee-safe” for everyone.

**Sources:** [NHS: knee pain and when to seek help](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/knee-pain/); [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/).

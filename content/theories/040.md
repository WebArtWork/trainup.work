## 40. Loaded Carries

### EN: Short
Loaded carries mean walking while you hold a weight, such as dumbbells or kettlebells. A carry is simple but hard work: it trains grip strength, trunk stability, posture and walking pattern together.

### EN: Detailed
**Common carry types.** A farmer’s carry uses one weight in each hand. A suitcase carry uses one weight on one side, so your trunk has to resist bending sideways. A front carry holds the load near your chest, and an overhead carry asks more of shoulder stability and needs more headroom. Each position changes the muscles and skill involved.

**Basic technique.** Choose a load you can hold securely for the whole walk, stand tall in a comfortable posture, and walk with deliberate steps. Keep the weight secure, don’t lean far toward or away from it, and keep breathing on a long walk. Slow down when you turn and keep your path clear.

**How to dose carries.** You count carries by distance or time instead of reps. For example, carry two dumbbells you can hold steadily for 20–30 seconds, rest, then repeat. When your grip and posture stay the same, add a little distance, time or weight. A heavier weight doesn’t help if it makes your steps hurried and uneven.

**Space and safety.** Check that your route has enough clear floor, a non-slip surface, and no people or objects in the way. Put the weights down under control, and don’t drop them. Skip overhead carries under a low ceiling or when you aren’t sure of your shoulder control. Always train one-sided carries on both sides.

**Using TrainUp.** Carries can fill a strength slot when you have the equipment and room to walk. Your plan checks the floor size you set in Profile. If your back or hands limit how much spinal load or grip work you can take, add that body area as a limitation in Profile so the plan can leave out exercises that don’t fit.

## 102. Training With Sensitive Wrists

### EN: Short
Your wrists may hurt when your hands carry your body weight or hold a load at an awkward angle. Changing the grip, the support or the exercise can sometimes ease the strain while you keep training the same movement pattern.

### EN: Detailed
**Where wrist strain comes from.** Push-ups and planks on flat palms bend the wrist back a long way. Dumbbell and barbell exercises add gripping and stabilizing work. Wrist discomfort has many possible causes, and the exercise alone can’t tell you which one it is.

**Change how your hands meet the floor.** Elevated push-ups put less of your weight on your arms. Stable push-up handles, or dumbbells secured so they can’t roll, let you keep the wrist straighter. Forearm planks take the pressure off your palms completely. Some people also find a neutral-grip dumbbell press more comfortable than a straight-bar press.

**Keep the changes safe.** Never use dumbbells that can roll as makeshift push-up handles. Check that supports are stable and the surface can’t slip. Don’t force a painful wrist into a position just because a technique cue calls it ideal. If a movement hurts, stop and find a comfortable alternative.

**Progress slowly.** Build up total reps and control before you try harder bodyweight leverage. See a doctor for new pain after a fall, severe swelling, deformity, tingling or lost strength. If wrist discomfort keeps going, get it checked by a professional, since TrainUp can’t diagnose it.

**Using TrainUp.** Add your wrists as a limitation in Profile and list your equipment. The plan then picks exercises that fit, which may include core work on your forearms. After a workout you can report pain with the body area and exercise, and use the notes to track how your wrists held up. TrainUp doesn’t build rehab routines.

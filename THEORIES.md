# TrainUp theories: list of 120 theories

Theory list for **TrainUp** (trainup.work), in the same shape as the other learning apps
(see `../biology.md`, sections 7 and 8). Use it as the input list when generating the theory
files with another model: one Markdown file per theory (`001.md` … `120.md`), short and
detailed versions, English and Ukrainian.

The list follows what the app already trains and stores:

- goals: general fitness, strength, conditioning;
- catalog: 113 exercises, categories strength / conditioning / mobility, movement patterns
  squat, hinge, lunge, push and pull (horizontal and vertical), core, isolation, conditioning, mobility;
- planner inputs: fitness level, equipment, usable space, ceiling, surface, noise, impact,
  axial spinal load, high-load body areas, coordination difficulty, sets, reps, rest, RPE feedback.

Scope limits (from README): no medical diagnosis, no rehabilitation programs, no diet plans or
calorie targets. Theories on pain, limitations, nutrition and special groups give general
education and say when to see a professional.

Steps of 20: #1–20, #21–40, #41–60, #61–80, #81–100, #101–120.

## 2. Scope of each theory

### Training foundations

1. **How Training Changes the Body.** Stress, recovery and adaptation cycle; supercompensation as a model, not a law; SAID principle; short-term vs long-term adaptations (nervous system first, muscle later).
2. **Components of Physical Fitness.** Strength, power, muscular endurance, cardiorespiratory endurance, mobility, balance, coordination, body composition; which TrainUp goal develops which.
3. **Training Goals.** What general fitness, strength and conditioning programs look like (rep ranges, rest, exercise mix); why one primary goal per block; how goals overlap.
4. **Fitness Levels.** Practical markers of beginner, intermediate and advanced (training age, technique, push-up/squat benchmarks); how level changes volume, exercise choice and progression speed.
5. **Readiness Check.** PAR-Q+ style questions; conditions that need a doctor's clearance (heart, blood pressure, recent surgery, pregnancy, new pain); the app does not diagnose.
6. **Physical Activity Guidelines.** WHO adult guidelines: 150–300 min moderate or 75–150 min vigorous aerobic per week, muscle strengthening on 2+ days, less sitting; guidelines for older adults and teens.
7. **Setting Realistic Training Goals.** SMART goals; process vs outcome goals; realistic rates of strength and body change for beginners; reviewing goals every 4–8 weeks.
8. **Consistency and Training Habits.** Habit cues, minimum viable workout, scheduling fixed days, reminders, handling missed sessions without "starting over".

### Body and movement

9. **Major Muscle Groups.** Chest, back, shoulders, biceps, triceps, core, glutes, quads, hamstrings, calves: location, main action, example exercises from the catalog.
10. **How Muscles Work.** Concentric, eccentric and isometric actions; agonist, antagonist, synergist, stabilizer; examples in a squat and a push-up.
11. **Muscle Fiber Types.** Type I and type II fibers; size principle of recruitment; why light loads near failure still recruit fast fibers; genetics vs trainability.
12. **Joints and Range of Motion.** Joint types relevant to training (hinge, ball and socket); full vs partial range; active vs passive range; why range is individual.
13. **The Spine, Core and Bracing.** Spinal curves, neutral spine, intra-abdominal pressure, bracing vs hollowing; what "core" means beyond abs.
14. **Energy Systems in Exercise.** Phosphocreatine, glycolytic and aerobic systems; approximate time domains; how they overlap; link to sets, intervals and rest.
15. **Heart Rate and Training Zones.** Resting and maximum heart rate; age-based estimates (220 − age, Tanaka 208 − 0.7 × age) and their error; zones by %HRmax and heart rate reserve (Karvonen); talk test.
16. **Breathing During Exercise.** Exhale on effort as a beginner rule; bracing and short breath holds for heavy lifts; Valsalva risks for people with high blood pressure; breathing in cardio.
17. **Motor Learning.** Stages of skill learning; practice quality, cues, feedback; why fatigue harms technique practice; learning new exercises early in a session.
18. **Muscle Growth (Hypertrophy).** Mechanical tension as main driver; role of volume, proximity to failure, protein and sleep; realistic rates of muscle gain.

### Training variables

19. **Sets, Reps and Rest Periods.** Definitions; typical rest by goal (strength 2–5 min, hypertrophy 1–3 min, endurance under 1 min); reading a plan line like 3 × 8–12, 90 s.
20. **Rep Ranges and What They Train.** Strength, hypertrophy and endurance continuum; why the ranges overlap; choosing ranges for beginners and for bodyweight exercises.
21. **Training Volume.** Volume as hard sets per muscle per week; volume load (sets × reps × weight); typical starting ranges (about 10 sets per muscle per week); how to raise volume gradually.
22. **Intensity: Load and Percentage of 1RM.** 1RM definition; %1RM vs reps possible table; why beginners should estimate, not test, a 1RM; Epley and Brzycki formulas.
23. **Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE).** Session RPE (CR-10) vs set RPE; how TrainUp asks for RPE after a session; how RPE guides next-session load; common rating errors.
24. **Reps in Reserve (RIR).** RIR scale and its link to RPE (RPE 8 ≈ 2 RIR); accuracy improves with experience; using RIR to choose load.
25. **Lifting Tempo.** Tempo notation (e.g. 3-1-1-0); slow eccentrics for control; when tempo matters and when it does not.
26. **Timed Sets and Holds.** Duration-based exercises (plank, wall sit, hang); how to progress time; holding good position vs just lasting.
27. **Training Frequency.** Sessions per week vs frequency per muscle; 2× per muscle per week as a practical default; fitting 2, 3, 4 or 5 days.
28. **Exercise Order in a Session.** Complex and multi-joint first, isolation later; high-skill and high-impact early; core and conditioning placement.
29. **Training to Failure.** Technical vs absolute failure; costs (fatigue, injury risk on complex lifts); safe exercises to push close to failure; 0–3 RIR guidance.
30. **Planning Session Length.** Estimating time from sets × set duration + rest + warm-up; trimming a session to fit 20, 30 or 45 minutes without losing the main work.

### Movement patterns

31. **Fundamental Movement Patterns.** Squat, hinge, lunge, push, pull, carry, core, locomotion; why a balanced program covers each; how TrainUp tags exercises.
32. **The Squat Pattern.** Stance, knee and hip path, depth, knees over toes myth; progressions from bodyweight squat to goblet and back squat; common faults.
33. **The Hip Hinge Pattern.** Hips back with a neutral spine; hinge vs squat; Romanian deadlift, kettlebell swing, glute bridge; teaching the hinge with a wall or dowel.
34. **Lunges and Single-Leg Training.** Reverse, split and walking lunges, Bulgarian split squat, step-up; balance demands; correcting side-to-side differences.
35. **Horizontal Pushing.** Push-up and bench press technique; scapular position; progressions wall → knee → full push-up → elevated feet.
36. **Vertical Pushing.** Overhead press technique; ribcage and lower back control; shoulder mobility requirements; pike push-up as a bodyweight option.
37. **Horizontal Pulling.** Rows with dumbbell, band, cable, table and towel; scapular retraction; torso angle and difficulty.
38. **Vertical Pulling.** Pull-up and lat pulldown; scapular pulls, negatives and band assistance as progressions; dead hang.
39. **Core Training.** Anti-extension (plank, dead bug), anti-rotation (Pallof press, bird dog), anti-lateral flexion (side plank, suitcase carry); why crunches are not the whole picture.
40. **Loaded Carries.** Farmer's, suitcase and front carries; grip, posture and core benefits; distance and time progressions.
41. **Isolation Exercises.** Curls, extensions, raises, leg curls and extensions; when isolation is useful; placing them after main lifts.
42. **Balancing Push and Pull.** Push-to-pull ratio; posture and shoulder health; balancing quads and hamstrings, front and back of the body.

### Program design

43. **Principles of Training.** Overload, specificity, individualization, progression, reversibility, variation, recovery.
44. **Progressive Overload.** Ways to progress: load, reps, sets, range, tempo, density, harder variation; progress one variable at a time.
45. **Double Progression.** Work up to the top of a rep range at a set load, then raise the load and drop to the bottom; worked example over several weeks.
46. **Full-Body, Upper/Lower and Split Programs.** Structure, pros and cons of each; which suits 2, 3, 4 or 5 days and which level.
47. **Structuring a Training Week.** Spreading sessions and muscle groups; rest days; placing hard and easy days; keeping 48 h between hard sessions for the same muscles.
48. **Periodization Basics.** Linear, undulating and block periodization explained simply; mesocycles and microcycles; why beginners need little periodization.
49. **Deload Weeks.** Purpose; signs a deload is needed; reducing volume by about 40–50% while keeping intensity; frequency (every 4–8 weeks or by need).
50. **Choosing Exercises.** Matching exercises to goal, level, equipment, space and limitations; stability vs freedom; staying with exercises long enough to progress.
51. **Progressions and Regressions.** Making an exercise easier or harder by leverage, range, support, load, tempo; examples from push-ups, squats and pull-ups.
52. **Supersets and Circuits.** Antagonist supersets, compound sets, circuits, EMOM and AMRAP formats; time savings vs fatigue costs.
53. **Training Plateaus.** Causes (recovery, sleep, too little or too much volume, same stimulus); checklist to break a plateau; when slower progress is normal.
54. **Detraining and Returning After a Break.** How fast strength and endurance decline; muscle memory; returning at about 50–70% of previous volume and building back.

### Equipment

55. **Bodyweight Training.** Strengths and limits; progressing without weights by leverage, unilateral work, tempo and range.
56. **Dumbbell Training.** Unilateral and range benefits; choosing dumbbell weights; safely getting dumbbells into position for presses.
57. **Kettlebell Training.** Offset load; ballistic vs grind movements; swing safety; choosing a starting kettlebell weight.
58. **Resistance Band Training.** Variable resistance; band levels and colors are not standard; anchoring safely; checking bands for wear.
59. **Barbell Training.** Bar, plates, collars; setting up in a rack with safety pins; when barbell work makes sense for a beginner.
60. **Machines and Cable Training.** Fixed path benefits; seat and pad setup; cables for constant tension; machines for people with limitations.
61. **Pull-Up Bar: Installation and Safety.** Doorframe vs wall/ceiling mounted bars; load ratings; testing before use; when the app treats the bar as unavailable.
62. **Benches, Steps and Jump Ropes.** Flat and adjustable bench uses; step height and stability; choosing rope length.
63. **Choosing the Right Load.** Picking a starting load by RIR; warm-up sets; adjusting load between sets; when the available weights are too light or heavy.
64. **Training With Household Substitutes.** Table rows, towel rows, chair dips, backpack load, water bottles; safety checks for furniture; why the app does not assume a custom item matches.

### Space and environment

65. **Usable Space and Exercise Clearance.** Measuring floor length and width; footprint of lying, lunging and jumping exercises; why "home" is not enough information.
66. **Ceiling Height and Overhead Exercises.** Clearance for presses, jumps and pull-ups; quick check with arms overhead; swaps when the ceiling is low.
67. **Training Surface and Footwear.** Hard floor, mat, carpet, grass, track; slip risk; flat vs cushioned shoes; barefoot training.
68. **Noise and Impact When Training at Home.** Quiet alternatives to jumps; low-impact versions of conditioning moves; dampening with mats; neighbor-friendly scheduling.
69. **Training in a Gym: Etiquette and Safety.** Re-racking, sharing equipment, collars, asking for a spot; planning around busy times.
70. **Training Outdoors: Weather and Terrain.** Heat, humidity and cold precautions; hydration; uneven ground and slopes; using park benches and bars safely.

### Conditioning

71. **Building an Aerobic Base.** Why easy aerobic work matters; zone 2 explained simply; weekly minutes targets; how long it takes to see change.
72. **Steady-State Cardio.** Walking, jogging, cycling, machines; choosing intensity by talk test or heart rate; progressing time before speed.
73. **Interval Training.** Work and recovery intervals; aerobic and anaerobic intervals; examples for walking, running and jump rope.
74. **HIIT and Tabata.** Definitions (Tabata 20 s / 10 s × 8); real intensity requirements; who should wait before doing HIIT; limits on weekly HIIT volume.
75. **Work-to-Rest Ratios.** Ratios like 1:3, 1:1, 2:1 and what each trains; choosing ratios by level.
76. **Low-Impact Conditioning.** Marching, step-ups, shadow boxing, incline walking, machine intervals; for joints, noise and beginners.
77. **Plyometrics and Jumping.** Landing mechanics; prerequisites (strength, pain-free landing); volume measured in foot contacts; progression.
78. **Running Technique Basics.** Posture, cadence, foot strike myths; build-up plans for beginners (walk-run intervals); sprints and accelerations safety.
79. **Jump Rope Skills.** Rope length, grip, small jumps on the balls of the feet; basic, boxer step, side-to-side; progressing duration.
80. **Combining Strength and Cardio.** Interference effect and why it is small for most people; ordering and separating sessions; concurrent training for general fitness.

### Warm-up and mobility

81. **The Warm-Up.** RAMP model (raise, activate, mobilize, potentiate); 5–10 minute general warm-up; ramp-up sets for the first exercise.
82. **The Cool-Down.** Gradual return to rest; what a cool-down does and does not do (soreness evidence); breathing and light mobility.
83. **Mobility and Flexibility.** Difference between them; when limited range really limits training; testing basic ranges (overhead reach, deep squat, hip flexors).
84. **Static and Dynamic Stretching.** When to use each; static stretching before strength work in moderation; hold times and frequency.
85. **Hip Mobility.** Hip flexor, adductor and rotation work; kneeling hip flexor stretch; impact on squats and hinges.
86. **Thoracic Spine and Shoulder Mobility.** Open book rotation, arm circles, cat-cow; links to overhead pressing and posture.

### Recovery

87. **Recovery Between Sessions.** Time course of muscle recovery; 48–72 h for the same muscle after hard work; signs of incomplete recovery.
88. **Sleep and Training.** Sleep needs (7–9 h for adults); effects of poor sleep on performance and injury risk; practical sleep habits.
89. **Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness.** Cause, timing (peaks 24–72 h), repeated bout effect; soreness is not a measure of a good workout; training while sore.
90. **Overreaching and Overtraining.** Functional vs non-functional overreaching; overtraining syndrome; warning signs; what to change.
91. **Hydration.** Fluid needs around training; thirst and urine color as guides; heat and long sessions; risks of overdrinking.
92. **Nutrition Basics for Active People.** General principles only: energy balance, protein (about 1.2–2.0 g/kg for active people), carbohydrates around training, whole foods; no meal plans or calorie targets; when to see a dietitian.
93. **Daily Readiness and Stress.** How life stress, sleep and illness affect training; adjusting a session on a bad day; the neck check for training while ill.
94. **Active Recovery.** Light movement on rest days; walking and mobility; how hard is too hard for active recovery.

### Safety and limitations

95. **Technique Before Load.** Why ego lifting fails; owning the movement at light load; filming yourself; stopping a set when form breaks.
96. **Pain or Discomfort: How to Tell the Difference.** Muscle effort and burning vs sharp, joint, radiating or lasting pain; the 0–10 pain scale and simple traffic-light rule; how TrainUp re-checks limitations after new pain.
97. **Warning Signs: When to Stop Immediately.** Chest pain, unusual shortness of breath, dizziness, fainting, palpitations, numbness, sudden severe pain; when to call emergency services.
98. **Axial Spinal Load.** What axial loading is; which exercises load the spine (back squat, overhead press, carries); lower-load alternatives; why the app limits it for some users.
99. **Training With Sensitive Knees.** General principles: tolerable range, load and impact; knee-friendly exercise options; when to see a professional. No diagnosis or rehab protocol.
100. **Training With a Sensitive Lower Back.** General principles: spine-friendly hinge and core work, avoiding provoking positions, gradual exposure; red flags.
101. **Training With Sensitive Shoulders.** Pressing angles, range, pulling balance; exercises often tolerated; red flags.
